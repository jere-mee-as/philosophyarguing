## Deontology | Ethics
> Some acts are wrong whatever the consequences
Pushed far enough, with thousands of lives against one small violation, almost everyone gives way. Then the rule was a strong weight, not an absolute.
So it's wrong to tell one lie to save a city. A theory that loses a city on a technicality has a bug.
Deontology is caring more about your own clean hands than about what happens to anyone.
> Rights are side constraints, not goals
If violating a right is so bad, it's puzzling why one may not violate one to prevent five identical violations. The constraint needs a ground other than the badness.
If rights matter, more rights respected is better than fewer. He's against that. He prefers the one he personally didn't touch.
The deontologist lets five rights be violated so his own record stays clean. It's ethics as a no-hitter.
> Intentions matter, not just outcomes
The line between intending a harm and merely foreseeing it is easy to redraw by redescribing the act, which makes it a poor guide.
The road to hell is paved with them. That's been known for a while.
"I didn't intend it, I just knew it would happen" has never worked on anyone's mother.
> Duties can conflict, and judgment decides
A list of duties with no ranking leaves the hard cases to intuition, which is where the theory was supposed to help.

Deontology has rules for everything except which rule.

## Moral relativism | Ethics

Moral relativists become universalists the moment somebody cuts in line.
> We shouldn't judge other societies
That's a universal moral rule about what everyone should do, which is what the theory says there aren't any of.

"Who are we to judge?" asks the person judging people who judge.

People disagreed about the shape of the earth. Disagreement shows that someone is wrong, or that the question is hard.

Relativism: every culture's values are valid, except the ones that say theirs are better, which is all of them.

## Nihilism | Ethics
> Nothing matters

Nihilism is the only philosophy where the argument for it is the reason not to bother.

## Hedonism | Ethics

Deception matters, but logistics, numbers and industry decide most wars. A theory centered on cleverness flatters the reader.
Wars are won by the side with more trucks. There's no chapter on trucks.
The Art of War is thirteen chapters of "be smarter than the other guy."
> The supreme art is to win without fighting
It's an attractive ideal with few instructions attached. Opponents who could be beaten without fighting weren't much of a problem to begin with.
If you can win without fighting, the other side was losing anyway. He's taking credit for the weather.
"Win without fighting" is great advice, right up there with "buy low, sell high."
> Know your enemy and know yourself
True and nearly empty. It tells a commander to have good information without saying how, which is the entire difficulty.
"Have all the information" is not strategy. It's what strategy is for when that didn't happen.
Know yourself, know your enemy, and you'll never lose. Unfalsifiable: if you lost, you didn't know.
> Its lessons apply to business and life
The maxims are general enough to fit anything, which is why they fit quarterly sales as easily as siege warfare.
A book that applies to war, marketing, dating and golf is a horoscope.
Sun Tzu may not have existed, which is the one piece of deception that definitely worked.

## The Bhagavad Gita | The East
> Act without attachment to the fruits of action
Complete indifference to results would make it impossible to choose between actions, since their results are how they differ.
If the results don't matter, there's no reason to do one thing over another. He's detached all the way to the couch.
Do your job and don't think about the outcome. Management loves this book.
> Do the duty of your station
The duty in question comes from birth into a class. The ethic tells each person to stay in their assigned role, which is what its critics have always pointed out.
"Better your own duty done badly than another's done well." Convenient for whoever handed out the duties.
The Gita's answer to "should I do this?" is "what were you born as?"
> Arjuna must fight, though it means killing his kin
The argument that the soul can't be killed, so killing isn't really killing, would excuse any killing whatever.
"Nobody really dies, so go ahead" is an argument that works for every war and every murder. It proves too much by a lot.
A man doesn't want to kill his family, and God talks him into it. That's the plot.
> There are many paths to the same goal
Paths of knowledge, devotion and action rest on different pictures of what's ultimately real. Calling them one goal papers over the difference.
If every path gets there, there's no reason to take the hard one, and the book is eighteen chapters of the hard ones.
Oppenheimer quoted it at the bomb test. That's the most famous application.

## Mindfulness | The East
> Paying attention to the present moment reduces suffering
The measured benefits are modest, about what other active treatments give, and the early studies were small and poorly controlled.
The studies say it works about as well as going for a walk. The walk is free and gets somewhere.
Mindfulness is noticing that you're stressed, for ten minutes, on an app with a subscription.
> It's Buddhism's insight without the religion
What was removed is the ethics and the goal. A technique meant to end attachment was repurposed to make people better at their attachments.
They kept the breathing and dropped the part about giving everything up. That's keeping the wrapper.
Mindfulness was designed to end the self, and got sold as self-care.
> Observe your thoughts without judging them
Sometimes thoughts deserve judgment, and distress is a signal about one's situation. Non-judgment can turn a real problem into a private attitude adjustment.
"Don't judge your thoughts" is a judgment about thoughts. He failed in the instructions.
If your job is making you miserable, mindfulness will help you notice that calmly and stay.
> It belongs in schools and workplaces
Offered by employers, it puts the burden of coping on the individual and leaves the conditions causing the stress as they were.
The company that causes the stress pays for the class about the stress. It's cheaper than fixing anything.
Corporate mindfulness: breathe through the layoffs.
