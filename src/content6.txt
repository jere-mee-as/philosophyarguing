## Existentialism | Continental
> We must create our own meaning
If meaning is whatever one chooses, no choice can be better than another, and the seriousness the movement demands has nothing to rest on.
If I get to make it up, I pick "naps." That's as valid as his, by his rules, and he hates that.
Existentialism is being a teenager, but in French and with citations.
> Authenticity is the highest value
Authenticity says be true to yourself and is silent on what self. A committed fanatic is as authentic as anyone.
Plenty of terrible people were being completely themselves. That was the problem.
"Authentic" is what people call themselves right before being rude.
> Anxiety reveals our freedom
Anxiety has ordinary causes, and reading a mood as a disclosure of metaphysical truth is a choice the argument doesn't justify.
Anxiety reveals too much coffee. It's revealed that for years and nobody built a philosophy on it.
They took a bad mood and gave it a Danish name and a German name.
> Existence can't be captured by any system
The claim is general and systematic, and the major existentialists wrote very large books laying out structures of existence.
"No system can capture it," Being and Nothingness, 700 pages, with a table of contents.
Existentialism rejected systems and then had required reading.

## Phenomenology | Continental
> Go back to the things themselves
The descriptions that result differ from one phenomenologist to the next, with no agreed way to tell whose account of experience is right.
Everyone went back to the things themselves and came home with a different thing.
Phenomenology is describing a coffee cup for forty pages and calling it rigor.
> Bracket the natural attitude and describe pure experience
Whether anyone can suspend their assumptions on command is doubtful, and there's no check on whether it was done. Husserl's own students mostly gave it up.
"Set aside all your assumptions" is an assumption about what can be set aside.
The epoché is closing your eyes and saying you've stopped assuming things.
> Consciousness is always consciousness of something
Moods, pains and dizziness don't obviously point at anything. The thesis holds for many mental states and is stretched to cover the rest.
A headache isn't about anything. It's just there, being a headache.
Intentionality: the discovery that when you think, you think about stuff.
> It's a rigorous science of experience
A science has shared methods and results others can check. A century on, phenomenology has schools and founders, and no body of settled findings.
A science where every practitioner gets different results from the same experiment is called a hobby.
Husserl kept restarting the whole project from scratch every few years. That's how the rigorous science was going.

## Levinas | Continental
> Ethics is first philosophy
Putting ethics before any account of what exists or what we can know leaves the ethical claim with nothing to be checked against.
Ethics can't be first. Somebody has to exist before anybody owes them anything. Order of operations.
Levinas put ethics before everything, including clarity.
> The face of the Other commands me
If the command comes before all concepts and reasons, there's no way to say what it asks or to weigh one face against another.
The face says "do not kill," apparently. Mostly faces say "do I know you?"
His whole ethics is making eye contact.
> My responsibility to the Other is infinite
An obligation without limit can't be met and can't guide a choice between claims. Something infinite in every direction recommends nothing in particular.
Infinite responsibility to each of eight billion people. He's behind.
"I am infinitely responsible for everyone" has never once gotten a dish washed.
> Western philosophy reduces the Other to the Same
The charge sweeps two and a half millennia into one gesture. Any tradition can be made to look uniform at that distance.
All of Western philosophy made the same mistake, and one man noticed. That's usually not how that goes.
Levinas says philosophy erases difference, in prose where every sentence sounds the same.

## Cioran | Continental
> It would have been better never to have been born
The claim compares existing with a state in which there's no one to be better off. And he lived to 84 without acting on it.
Better for whom? There's no one in the other column. He's comparing his life to an empty chair and losing.
Cioran thought being born was the catastrophe and lived to 84 in Paris on a nice street.
> Consciousness is a disease
"Disease" assumes a healthy state for comparison, and the only vantage from which to make the comparison is the supposedly diseased one.
If consciousness is a disease, so is the thought that it's a disease. It's a symptom reviewing itself.
He diagnosed consciousness as an illness using consciousness, and didn't get a second opinion.
> Lucidity means seeing that everything is futile
Pessimism is presented as clear sight and optimism as illusion, with no argument that the dark mood is the accurate one.
Being miserable isn't the same as being right. Lots of people manage only one.
Cioran is what happens when insomnia gets a book deal.
> Only the aphorism is honest; systems lie
A scattered form protects claims from having to cohere. That's convenient, and it isn't the same thing as honesty.
A man who never writes more than three sentences in a row can't be caught contradicting himself. That's the method.
Aphorisms: for when an argument would take effort.

## Dostoevsky | Continental
> Without God, everything is permitted
People without belief hold and act on moral norms, and belief has licensed plenty. Whatever grounds morality, the psychology doesn't go the way the line predicts.
Then with God, nothing bad would have been done by believers. Somebody should check the record on that.
"Without God everything is permitted" is said in the novel by a character, and people quote it like he signed it.
> Reason alone leads to the Grand Inquisitor
The novels dramatize what rationalists do in Dostoevsky's imagination. A story built to show a view failing isn't evidence that it fails.
He wrote the atheists himself and then they lost. That's not an argument, that's being the author.
Every rationalist in Dostoevsky goes mad or kills someone. It's a pattern, and the pattern is the novelist.
> Suffering redeems
Much suffering crushes people and teaches nothing. Finding redemption in it risks telling the wretched their condition is good for them.
If suffering redeemed, the worst places on earth would be full of saints. They're full of suffering.
Suffering ennobles, wrote a man who gambled away the rent and wrote about that too.
> The Underground Man refutes rational egoism
He shows that people can act against their interest out of spite, which is a fact about perversity, not a refutation of a theory about what's good.
A man doing something stupid to prove he's free has proved one of those two things.
Notes from Underground is the first blog.

## Frankfurt School | Theory
> The culture industry manufactures conformity
Audiences interpret, resist and repurpose what they consume. The picture of passive masses was asserted from above, with little study of actual audiences.
People watched a lot of movies and then had the 1960s. The conformity machine had a recall.
Adorno heard jazz and concluded civilization was over.
> Enlightenment reason turns into domination
If reason as such leads to domination, the critique, which is an exercise of reason, has no ground to stand on. Habermas made this point against his own teachers.
They used reason to show reason is a trap. So either the argument's a trap or it isn't true.
Dialectic of Enlightenment: two men in California explaining that everything is fascism, including the weather.
> Critical theory unites analysis with emancipation
Building the desired political result into the method means the theory can't discover that it's wrong about what would free anyone.
A theory that knows its conclusion before it starts is called a position.
They theorized revolution for forty years, and when students actually occupied the institute, Adorno called the police.
> Tolerance of the intolerable is repressive
A theory that licenses suppressing views the theorist judges regressive hands a weapon to whoever holds power, whichever direction they face.
Tolerance means only tolerating the correct side. He needed a whole essay because it doesn't sound good short.
Marcuse figured out that free speech is oppression unless he's the one talking.

## Baudrillard | Theory
> We live in simulation; the real has disappeared
The claim trades on the difference between the real and its images to announce that there's no difference. If it were true it couldn't be stated.
If the real had disappeared, he couldn't know what was missing. He's describing the thing he says is gone.
Baudrillard announced that reality was over and kept accepting real money for it.
> Signs now refer only to other signs
People still get wet in the rain. That media shape perception doesn't show that nothing lies behind the images.
A stop sign refers to stopping. People who treat it as referring to other signs are in the hospital.
Hyperreality is noticing that Disneyland is fake and then not stopping.
> The Gulf War did not take place
Read as media criticism it says the public saw a staged version. Read as written, it's false and offensive to the people it happened to.
There were casualties. They took place.
He said a war didn't happen while it was on television, and his defenders say you have to read it carefully.
> The Matrix is based on his work
He said the film misunderstood him. On his view there's no outside to escape to, which is the film's entire plot.
Baudrillard said The Matrix got him wrong. The movie made more sense, so that checks out.
The book appears in The Matrix for four seconds, hollowed out, used as a box. Fair.

## Lacan | Theory
> The unconscious is structured like a language
The slogan borrows linguistic terms loosely, and working linguists don't recognize the borrowed concepts in the use he makes of them.
Languages have grammar and can be learned. Nobody has ever learned to speak unconscious.
Lacan said the unconscious is structured like a language, specifically one that only he spoke.
> The mirror stage founds the ego
The developmental claim was made with almost no evidence, and children blind from birth form a sense of self without mirrors.
People had selves before mirrors were invented. Ponds are not that reliable.
A baby looks in a mirror, and from that he got a theory of everything and fifty years of seminars.
> Desire is the desire of the Other
The phrase supports several incompatible readings, and the theory doesn't choose. What can't be pinned down can't be tested or applied.
I want a sandwich. Nobody else is involved.
Every Lacanian sentence means three things and is defended with whichever one wasn't attacked.
> The Real resists symbolization
A category defined as what can't be said or imagined does no explanatory work and can't be wrong.
If it resists symbolization, stop writing about it. He wrote about it for thirty years.
The Real is whatever the theory can't explain, renamed as the theory's deepest concept.

## Jung | Theory
> There's a collective unconscious shared by all humanity
Shared themes in myths are explained by shared biology, common life situations and contact between cultures. An inherited store of images adds nothing testable.
Every culture has stories about mothers because everybody has a mother. No psychic underground required.
Jung found the same symbols in every culture, having gone looking for them.
> Archetypes shape our psychology
The archetypes are vague enough to fit any story or dream, and no finding could count against their presence.
A wise old man, a trickster, a mother. That's not the structure of the psyche, that's casting.
Archetypes are zodiac signs for people who went to graduate school.
> Synchronicity: meaningful coincidences aren't mere chance
With enough events, striking coincidences are expected. Calling them meaningful describes how they feel and adds no connection between them.
Thinking of someone right before they call happens. So do the nine thousand times they didn't call.
Synchronicity is noticing a coincidence and refusing to do the math.
> Individuation is the goal of psychological life
The stages and their end state come from his own experience and his reading of alchemy. There's no evidence that minds develop along that path.
He based the map of the human soul on his own dreams and some medieval chemistry books.
Jung gave us the Myers-Briggs. That's the testable part, and it failed.

## Structuralism | Theory
> Meaning comes from differences within a system
That signs contrast with each other doesn't show the world plays no part in what they mean. Words are also used to pick things out.
"Cat" means cat because of cats. The differences help. The cats help more.
Structuralism is the theory that a dictionary could be written without anyone going outside.
> The sign is arbitrary
The sound is arbitrary. What follows from that is modest, and it was stretched into the claim that categories and facts are arbitrary too.
The word "poison" is arbitrary. The poison isn't. He confused the label with the bottle.
Saussure never wrote the book. His students assembled it from lecture notes after he died. The foundation is somebody's homework.
> Myths and kinship share universal deep structures
The analyses can't be replicated: different analysts find different structures in the same myth, with no procedure for deciding between them.
He found binary oppositions in every myth. Give anyone two columns and a myth and they will too.
Lévi-Strauss reduced world mythology to raw and cooked, and people let him.
> The subject is an effect of structures
If people are produced by structures all the way down, the theorist is too, and the theory is one more effect with no claim to have seen the truth.
Then the structures wrote his book, and they should get the royalties.
Structuralism abolished the author, and every structuralist put their name on the cover.

## Mark Fisher | Theory
> It's easier to imagine the end of the world than the end of capitalism
That says something about imagination and film budgets. People imagined alternatives constantly, and the hard part was showing they'd work.
People imagine the end of capitalism all the time. There are whole bookstores. What's hard is the second chapter.
"Capitalist realism" is a name for not having a plan.
> Capitalism has absorbed all resistance
A theory on which every protest is absorbed and every success is co-optation can't be told apart from one where things simply aren't that bad.
If all resistance gets absorbed, so did the book. It's sold by a multinational and comes with free shipping.
Capitalist Realism is eighty pages on how nothing can be done, and it sold very well.
> Culture has stalled: nothing new, only retro
Every generation's critics say this about the music after their youth. Novelty is easier to see in retrospect and in scenes one isn't part of.
Every forty-year-old thinks music stopped being new when he was twenty-five. He wrote it up.
Hauntology is being sad that the future from old TV shows didn't arrive.
> Mental distress is political, not just personal
Social conditions matter, but making every case a symptom of the economy tells people nothing useful about their own, and it isn't supported in that strong form.
Unhappiness predates capitalism by a few thousand years. The Book of Job has no employers in it.
Everything is capitalism's fault, a claim that's very available on mugs.

## Accelerationism | Theory
> Speed capitalism up to push through it
Nothing shows that intensifying a system produces its successor and not just more of it, or a collapse nobody would choose.
"Make it worse until it gets better" has been tried by every gambler.
Accelerationism: the plan is to floor it and find out what the wall is made of.
> Capital is an inhuman intelligence using us
Markets are aggregates of human choices under rules humans set. Treating the aggregate as an agent with aims is a metaphor promoted to a theory.
Capital doesn't want anything. It's money. He got scared of a spreadsheet.
Nick Land wrote that capitalism is an alien intelligence from the future, and people in tech said "finally, someone gets it."
> Technology's growth can't and shouldn't be steered
Technologies have been steered, slowed and banned many times. "Inevitable" tends to be said by those who'd profit from nobody trying.
Human cloning, chemical weapons, lead in gasoline. Steered. It happens constantly.
e/acc is "you can't stop progress" from people who own the progress.
> Slowing down is the real risk
Speed and safety trade off in every engineered system. Asserting that caution is always the greater danger is a slogan, not an analysis.
Every engineer who ever lived would like a word about "slowing down is the risk."
The movement that wants to accelerate everything has been mostly posting for ten years.

## Byung-Chul Han | Theory
> We're a burnout society, exploiting ourselves
Most overwork still has an employer, a mortgage or a visa behind it. Describing it as self-exploitation takes the pressure off whoever's applying it.
People aren't exploiting themselves. They have a boss. He can be reached by email, at midnight, that's the issue.
The Burnout Society is sixty pages, because the reader is too tired for more. He knows his market.
> Transparency destroys trust and depth
The essays assert broad cultural diagnoses without evidence, and the opposite claim, that secrecy protects abuse, has at least as much support.
Opaque institutions have an excellent record, he said, naming none.
Han is against transparency and proves it with his prose.
> Digital life has abolished the Other
"The Other has disappeared" is the sort of sentence that can't be checked. People online meet more strangers and more disagreement than any generation before.
The internet is nothing but other people. That's the complaint everyone actually has.
He writes that we've lost the capacity for contemplation, in books designed to be read on a train.
> We've lost ritual and the ability to linger
Nostalgia for a slower, more ritual past rarely specifies when that was or who got to enjoy the lingering.
The people who had time to linger had servants. Somebody else was not lingering.
Byung-Chul Han publishes the same short book about once a year. It's a ritual.

## Locke | Political Thinkers
> Mix your labor with something and it's yours
Nozick's question: if I pour my tomato juice into the sea, do I own the sea or lose my juice? Why mixing labor yields ownership is never shown.
I painted a fence once. I don't own the fence. I asked.
Locke's theory of property is that whoever got there first and did some gardening owns it forever.
> Government rests on the consent of the governed
Almost nobody has consented. Tacit consent by staying put, as Hume said, is like saying a man pressed onto a ship consents by not jumping overboard.
Nobody asked me. Nobody asked anyone. The consent is theoretical and the taxes aren't.
Tacit consent: you agreed by being here. Check the terms and conditions of standing on a road.
> There's a right to revolution when government breaks its trust
With no judge of when the trust is broken except the people rebelling, the right amounts to: revolutions are justified when they succeed.
Who decides the trust was broken? Whoever wins. So it's a right that's awarded afterwards, like a trophy.
Locke defended the right to revolution, published it anonymously, and denied writing it until he died.
> Toleration should extend to all peaceful beliefs
His own letter excludes Catholics and atheists. The principle is narrower in his hands than in his admirers'.
All beliefs should be tolerated, except two, which were the two he didn't like.
The philosopher of liberty held shares in the Royal African Company and helped write a slave constitution for Carolina.

## Mill | Political Thinkers
> Power may be used over someone only to prevent harm to others
"Harm" is the contested term. Almost any conduct affects others somehow, so the principle settles little until harm is defined, and Mill doesn't.
Everything affects somebody. Under the harm principle either nothing's allowed or the word "harm" is doing a quiet shift.
The harm principle is "mind your own business," with exceptions to be announced.
> Free discussion leads to truth
Markets in ideas reward what's vivid and flattering as much as what's true. The evidence that open debate reliably sorts them is thin.
The internet is the freest discussion in history. Check on how the truth is doing.
Mill thought the best argument wins. He'd never seen a comment section.
> Experiments in living benefit everyone
Some experiments impose costs on children and neighbors who didn't volunteer, and a society can reasonably decline to run them all.
Experiments have control groups and ethics boards. He meant "let me do what I want," with a lab coat on it.
On Liberty: everyone should be free to live as they like, by a man who spent his career administering India.
> Better Socrates dissatisfied than a fool satisfied
The judges are people who've known both pleasures, but someone formed by one kind of life can't fairly sample the other. The test is rigged toward the examiner.
The fool is satisfied. That's the whole point of being satisfied. Mill lost and wrote it down as a win.
"Better to be Socrates dissatisfied" is a sentence only ever said by people who think they're Socrates.

## Adam Smith | Political Thinkers
> The invisible hand turns self-interest into public good
It does under conditions he listed and his admirers skip: competition, information and no power to rig the game. He was scathing about merchants.
If self-interest produced public good, burglars would be philanthropists.
The invisible hand appears once in The Wealth of Nations. One time. In a book of a thousand pages.
> The division of labor is the source of wealth
He also wrote that it makes the worker as stupid and ignorant as a human can become, and called for public schooling to offset it.
He said the pin factory makes pins faster and makes the men idiots. Same book. Nobody quotes the second half.
Smith's proof of capitalism is a factory that makes pins.
> Markets work best left alone
He supported regulation of banks, usury limits, public works and taxes that fall on the rich. The laissez-faire Smith is a later invention.
Smith wanted bank regulation and progressive taxes. His fans haven't read him. He's the Marx of the right.
Everyone who cites Adam Smith has read the same one paragraph.
> Sympathy is the basis of morality
Sympathy is partial and fades with distance. He patches it with an imagined impartial spectator, who brings in a standard sympathy doesn't supply.
We sympathize with people like us. So it's a theory of being nice to your friends, which was not in short supply.
The father of economics lived with his mother.

## Hayek | Political Thinkers
> No planner can have the knowledge that prices carry
Prices miss what isn't traded, like pollution and future generations, and large firms plan internally at vast scale without collapsing.
Walmart is a planned economy bigger than most countries. It plans every shelf. Somebody tell the knowledge problem.
Hayek proved central planning is impossible, and then logistics software happened.
> Economic planning is the road to serfdom
Postwar Western Europe built large welfare states with heavy planning and stayed free. The slide he predicted didn't happen.
Sweden's been on the road to serfdom for ninety years. Lovely road. Bike lanes.
The Road to Serfdom predicted Britain would turn totalitarian. It got the NHS.
> Social order emerges spontaneously, without design
Markets rest on designed institutions: property law, courts, central banks. "Spontaneous" order had a lot of drafting behind it.
Nothing about a stock exchange is spontaneous. It has a rulebook and a building.
Spontaneous order: nobody planned it, except all the people who wrote the laws.
> "Social justice" is a mirage
That no one intended a distribution doesn't make it beyond assessment. We judge unintended outcomes of systems we could change all the time.
Nobody intended traffic deaths either, and there's still a department for it.
He said liberty mattered most, and he preferred a liberal dictator to a democracy that wasn't liberal. He said that about Chile, out loud.

## Arendt | Political Thinkers
> Evil can be banal
Later research suggests Eichmann was a committed ideologue who performed the dull clerk at trial. The thesis may fit some functionaries and not its famous example.
The recordings came out. He was a true believer and proud of it. She watched a performance and reviewed it as real.
The banality of evil is the most quoted idea ever based on misjudging one man in a glass booth.
> Totalitarianism is a wholly new form of government
Putting Nazism and Stalinism under one concept flattens deep differences in ideology and structure, and historians of each have resisted it.
Every tyranny in history felt new to the people in it. She had a better publisher.
"Unprecedented" is what every generation calls its own disasters.
> Politics is action among equals, distinct from the social
Separating the political from questions of poverty and work leaves politics oddly empty, and she was criticized for what that separation made her say about schools and welfare.
Politics that isn't about who gets what is a book club.
Arendt's ideal politics is ancient Athens, minus the part where most people there couldn't take part.
> Thinking is what guards against evil
Highly educated, reflective people have joined and led terrible movements. Whatever protects against that, thoughtfulness alone isn't it.
Plenty of the people in charge had doctorates. Some had read philosophy. One had taught her.
"Evil comes from not thinking," wrote the best student of a man who thought a great deal and joined anyway.

## Carl Schmitt | Political Thinkers
> Sovereign is he who decides on the exception
Constitutional states handle emergencies under rules, with review afterwards. That someone could seize the decision doesn't make the seizure the essence of law.
So the law is whoever breaks it most successfully. Every coup leader agrees with him, which should bother him more than it did.
Schmitt's theory is that the rules only count until somebody important doesn't feel like it.
> Politics is the distinction between friend and enemy
That describes politics at its worst and leaves out bargaining, coalition and persuasion, which is most of what it is.
If politics is friends against enemies, a city council meeting about parking isn't politics. That's most of politics.
The friend–enemy distinction is high school with armies.
> Liberalism only postpones decisions with endless talk
Talk and procedure are how decisions get made without bloodshed. That it's slow is the cost of that, not evidence that it's empty.
The alternative to endless discussion was tried in his country, in his lifetime, with his help.
He complained that parliaments just talk, and then found out what the other option was.
> His diagnosis stands apart from his politics
He joined the Nazi party in 1933, defended the purge killings in print, and never recanted. The theory of the exception was the one he applied.
He had a theory about what politics is and then a chance to use it, and he joined the party in 1933.
People cite Schmitt "for the critique," the way people read certain magazines for the articles.

## Nationalism | Politics
> Every nation deserves its own state
Peoples are intermingled and nations overlap, so the principle applied anywhere creates new minorities, and has done so at great cost.
There are several thousand peoples and about two hundred states. Somebody's doing the map in pen.
Nationalism: the people on this side of the river are completely different from the ones over there. Look, there's a line.
> Nations are ancient, natural communities
Most national identities were assembled in the last two centuries by schools, armies and newspapers, with traditions often invented for the purpose.
Most "ancient national traditions" date to about 1850. The kilt pattern was a marketing decision.
Every nation's eternal identity was invented around the time of the railway.
> We owe more to compatriots than to strangers
Special ties may justify some priority, but birthplace is as arbitrary as any trait, and the priority is rarely kept within decent bounds.
He owes more to a stranger four hundred miles north than to one four miles east, because of a river.
Patriotism is the belief that your country is the best because you were born in it.
> Shared identity is what makes solidarity possible
Solidarity also forms around class, religion, city and cause, and national feeling has divided societies as often as it has bound them.
The country with the strongest national feeling isn't the one with the best welfare state. Check any list.
National unity is mostly agreeing on who doesn't count.

## Social democracy | Politics
> Capitalism can be tamed by the welfare state
The arrangement depended on unusual postwar conditions: fast growth, strong unions and capital that couldn't leave. All three have weakened.
Capital can move now and the welfare state can't. It's a leash where the dog holds the other end.
Social democracy is capitalism with an apology.
> The Nordic model can be adopted anywhere
It grew out of small, high-trust countries with strong unions and broad taxes on ordinary earners. Copying the spending without the rest hasn't worked well.
Step one is to be five million people who all trust each other and have oil. Step two is easy.
"Why can't we be like Denmark?" Because Denmark is full of Danes who pay 25% sales tax and don't complain.
> High taxes and strong growth can coexist
They have, in some places, with tax systems that fall heavily on consumption and wages. That's not the version usually on offer.
The Nordics fund it by taxing the middle class a lot. That part gets left out of the brochure.
The plan is Scandinavian services at American tax rates, and nobody will say which half is the joke.
> Reform beats revolution
Reforms can be reversed as soon as the balance of power shifts, and many have been. What's granted without changing ownership stays revocable.
Every reform is one election from being undone. They've been rebuilding the same sandcastle since 1945.
Social democrats have been one more election away from socialism for a hundred years.

## Centrism | Politics
> The truth is usually somewhere in the middle
The midpoint depends on where the extremes happen to be, so it moves with whoever shouts loudest. It isn't a method for finding anything.
One guy says two plus two is four, another says six. The centrist says five and feels mature.
Centrism is having no opinions, strongly.
> Both sides have a point
Sometimes. As a standing rule it's a way to avoid assessing which points are true, and symmetry is rarely what the evidence shows.
Both sides had a point about whether the earth moves, too. One of the points was wrong.
"Both sides" is what people say when they haven't read either.
> Moderation and compromise are political virtues
A compromise between a good policy and a bad one is half a bad policy. Whether to split the difference depends on what's being split.
Half a bridge is a compromise. Ask how it's working for traffic.
The compromise position on everything is to do it, but badly.
> Extremes on both ends resemble each other
The comparison picks out style, such as certainty and anger, and ignores content. What people want to do with power matters more than how loudly they want it.
Being very sure is not a political position. Abolitionists were very sure.
Horseshoe theory is how someone who knows nothing about politics explains all of it.

## Populism | Politics
> The people against the corrupt elite
"The people" is never everyone. It's a part claiming to be the whole, and those outside it become illegitimate by definition.
Every populist leader is a millionaire explaining who the real elites are.
Populism is the elite's favorite way of running against the elite.
> Ordinary people's common sense beats expertise
Common sense is local and often wrong about systems nobody experiences directly, like trade, epidemics or monetary policy.
Common sense is great until the plane needs landing.
"I trust regular people over experts," he says, to his doctor.
> A strong leader can express the popular will
There's no single popular will to express, and a leader who claims to embody it has a standing excuse to override courts, press and elections.
If he is the will of the people, anyone against him is against the people. It's a nice trick and it's the whole trick.
The voice of the common man always seems to own several homes.
> Elites and institutions are rigged against us
Sometimes they are, in specific ways that can be shown and fixed. As a blanket claim it can't be tested, since every counterexample is part of the rigging.
If everything is rigged, the election he won was too. He never mentions that one.
The system is rigged, say people the system keeps electing.

## Technocracy | Politics
> Experts should make policy
Policy turns on values and trade-offs as well as facts. Expertise says what will happen, not which outcome to want.
Experts can say what a tax will do. They can't say whether to want that. That's the part with all the fighting.
Technocracy: rule by the people who were confident about the last crisis.
> Policy questions have technical answers
Framing a choice as technical hides the value judgment inside it and removes it from the people who'll live with it.
"It's not political, it's just the optimal solution" is the most political sentence there is.
Every technocrat thinks politics is a math problem and voters are the rounding error.
> Voters are too uninformed to decide
Experts have their own blind spots and interests, and without accountability to the public nothing corrects them.
The experts were informed in 2008. Very informed. They had models.
"People are too dumb to vote" is always said by someone who's sure he'd be on the committee.
> Independent agencies beat elected politicians
Insulation from voters is also insulation from correction. Agencies drift toward the industries they regulate or toward their own convenience.
An agency nobody can vote out is called a what, in any other context?
Technocrats want government run like a business, by people who've never run one.

## Meritocracy | Politics
> Positions should go to the most able
"Ability" is measured after unequal upbringing, schooling and luck have done their work. The contest ratifies the head start.
The most able are, by remarkable coincidence, the children of the last most able.
Meritocracy is aristocracy with an entrance exam the aristocrats' kids are tutored for.
> Success reflects talent and effort
Luck in genes, family, timing and country accounts for much of any outcome. Effort matters, and the capacity for it is partly inherited too.
Every successful person worked hard. So did the guy who delivers their groceries.
"I earned everything I have," says a man who chose his parents very carefully.
> Equal opportunity makes unequal outcomes fair
Opportunities are never equal, and one generation's outcomes are the next one's starting line.
The race is fair, and some people start at the finish, and their kids start past it.
Equal opportunity: everyone is free to compete with people who had tutors since age three.
> A meritocratic elite governs better
An elite convinced it earned its place owes nothing to anyone, and tends to read everyone else's situation as deserved.
The old aristocrats at least knew it was luck.
The word "meritocracy" was coined in a satire warning against it. They took it as a business plan.

## Georgism | Politics
> Land value belongs to the community
Assessing land separately from buildings is hard and contestable, and a full tax would wipe out the savings of ordinary owners who paid market price.
The retiree who bought her house in 1980 is not a feudal lord. The tax can't tell the difference.
Georgists have one idea, and they would like to tell you about it.
> A single tax on land could fund government
Total land rents fall well short of modern public spending. It could be a good tax among several and still not be the only one.
The math worked in 1879, when government was a post office and a navy.
The single tax has been the obvious solution to everything since 1879 and has been adopted by nowhere.
> A land tax has no deadweight loss
In theory, if assessed perfectly. In practice, valuation errors and politics bring back the distortions the theory assumes away.
"No distortions, assuming perfect assessment." Assuming perfect anything, every tax is fine.
Economists all agree the land value tax is the best tax, which is how you know it'll never happen.
> Land speculation is the root of poverty
Poverty has many causes. Housing costs matter a great deal in some cities, and that doesn't make land the master key to the rest.
There were poor people before there were landlords. They were called everyone.
Monopoly was invented to teach Georgism. It's now a game about loving being the landlord.

## Neoreaction | Politics
> Democracy is a failed experiment
Democracies have the best record on famine, mass killing by the state and long-run prosperity. The comparison class is what matters.
The failed experiment contains every country he'd agree to live in.
Neoreaction: a man with a blog has figured out that kings were good.
> A sovereign CEO would govern better
Companies can fail and be replaced, and nobody is forced to stay. A state run that way has the powers of a CEO and none of the checks.
CEOs get fired by boards. He left out the board. Without it that's just a guy.
The plan is to run the country like a startup. Nine in ten startups fail, and usually nobody dies.
> "The Cathedral" of universities and media runs society
The theory treats decentralized agreement among educated people as a ruling body, and can't be wrong, since dissent within it is also the Cathedral.
An institution with no leader, no members list and no meetings is called "a lot of people disagreeing with him."
The Cathedral controls everything except, somehow, the people writing about the Cathedral for major publications.
> People should exit rather than have a voice
Exit is cheap for the mobile and rich and close to impossible for everyone else. A world of exit without voice is a world run for those who can leave.
"If you don't like it, leave" is not a political philosophy. It's a thing dads say.
Monarchists never picture themselves as the peasant. Not once. Not one of them.

## Pacifism | Politics
> Violence is never justified
Refusing force against an aggressor leaves the victims to pay for one's principles. Clean hands purchased with others' lives isn't obviously the moral position.
Somebody's breaking into a house. The pacifist has a position, and the family has a problem.
Pacifism works as long as someone else isn't one.
> Nonviolent resistance works better than force
The cases where it worked involved opponents with a press and a public that could be shamed. Against regimes with neither, the record is grim.
It worked on the British, who had newspapers. Ask how it goes without the newspapers.
Gandhi's advice to those facing the Nazis was nonviolence. He gave it. It's in print.
> War only breeds more war
Some wars ended things: slavery in one country, a genocide in another. Others bred more. The record is mixed, and the slogan isn't.
Germany and Japan have been at peace for eighty years. Something bred that.
"War never solved anything," except the ones in all the history books.
> Refusing to fight keeps one's hands clean
Standing aside is also a choice with consequences. The appeal to clean hands puts the pacifist's purity above the outcome for others.
Hands are clean. Everything else is on fire.
Pacifists are protected by people who aren't, and are very brave about it.

## Free speech | Politics
> The answer to bad speech is more speech
Lies spread faster than corrections, and some speech works by intimidation, which more speech from its targets doesn't answer.
A lie gets around the world while the more-speech is still looking for its keys.
"The answer to speech is more speech" is said by people with large platforms about people with none.
> Truth wins out in the marketplace of ideas
Markets deliver what sells. There's no reason the true idea should be the popular one, and plenty of history where it wasn't.
Markets also gave us the pet rock. Whatever's winning, it's what people buy.
The marketplace of ideas has a bestseller list and it is mostly horoscopes.
> Any restriction is a slippery slope
Every legal system restricts fraud, threats, perjury and libel without sliding into tyranny. Lines get drawn and held all the time.
Fraud is banned. Perjury is banned. The slope has been right there for three hundred years and nobody's slid.
Free speech absolutists have a list of exceptions. It's just shorter than yours and they made it.
> Offense is not harm
Some speech does more than offend: it targets, excludes and silences. The line between offense and harm is real and less tidy than the slogan.
"Words can't hurt anyone," he posts, furious about something someone said.
The loudest defenders of free speech block the most people.

## Universal basic income | Politics
> A basic income would end poverty
At a level that ends poverty the cost is enormous, and at an affordable level it's too small to live on. Pilots can't test the financing, since they don't tax the participants.
Multiply any real amount by the population. Then look at the budget. Then stop.
UBI: every pilot is a success, and every pilot was paid for by somebody else.
> People won't stop working
Short pilots can't show what a permanent, universal guarantee would do, since people behave differently when they know it will end.
A two-year study where everyone knows it stops in two years. Of course they kept their jobs.
"People will use the free time to make art." There's a lot of evidence about what people do with free time.
> It's simpler and less paternalistic than welfare
A flat payment gives the same to people with very different needs. Keeping extra programs for disability and housing brings the complexity back.
One check replaces everything, except all the things it can't replace, which stay.
It's the only policy loved by both libertarians and socialists, and each thinks the other hasn't read it.
> Automation will make it necessary
The prediction that machines will end work has been made for two centuries, and employment has kept shifting, not vanishing. It may be different this time, and that isn't known.
The robots have been about to take all the jobs since the loom.
Tech executives love UBI. It's the tip jar for the economy they're planning.
