## Arguments for God | Religion
> Whatever begins to exist has a cause
The premise comes from things that begin inside time out of earlier material. Whether it applies to time itself is unclear, and a cause is not yet a personal God.
Then God needs a cause. If God doesn't, the rule has an exception, and the universe would like to apply for it.
Who made God?
> Fine-tuning points to design
There's no known spread of possible constants against which ours are unlikely, selection effects and many universes are rival explanations, and a designer's wishes are assumed.
The universe is 99.999% vacuum that kills people instantly. If it's tuned for life, somebody should check the tuner.
The puddle thinks the hole was made for it.
> A maximally great being must exist
Kant: existing isn't a feature that makes a concept greater. And the modern version's key premise is as disputable as its opposite, which proves atheism by the same steps.
Imagine the greatest possible pizza. It would be greater if it existed. Check the kitchen.
A monk did this with an island in the eleventh century and it's been over since.
> Objective morality requires God
The Euthyphro problem: if good is good because God commands it, it's arbitrary, and if God commands it because it's good, the standard doesn't depend on God.
Then the most religious countries would have the least crime. Somebody should look that up before using the argument again.
Atheists somehow manage not to murder anyone without being watched.
> Religious experience is evidence
Experiences in different traditions support incompatible doctrines, and they can be brought on by known means, which weakens them as evidence.
People experience Elvis. The standard that gets God also gets Elvis, and Elvis has more sightings a year.
Five proofs, and each one needs faith to work.

## Atheism | Religion
> There's no evidence for God
What counts as evidence is the dispute. The cosmological and fine-tuning arguments are evidence in the ordinary sense, even if judged not enough.
There's no evidence against, either. Fifty-fifty. And billions of people picked one side, which breaks the tie.
Atheism takes more faith.
> The burden of proof is on the believer
Burden rules are conventions of conversation. Whoever asserts takes one on, including "there is no God," and "lack of belief" definitions dodge that.
The believer was here first. Whoever turns up second with a new claim has the burden, and atheism turned up around 1750.
"Lack of belief" is a position that somehow needs conventions, podcasts and a logo.
> Science has made God unnecessary
Science explains processes inside nature. Why there's a lawful nature at all isn't a question it's built to answer, so "unnecessary" goes too far.
Science was invented by monks, and the Big Bang was a priest's idea. Atheism is using the church's own equipment against it.
Newton was religious. So was the priest who came up with the Big Bang.
> Religion does more harm than good
Most of the research ties religious practice to giving, well-being and community. The harms are real, and the net claim isn't supported.
The twentieth century ran the experiment with officially atheist states. It isn't a study people like to cite.
Stalin was an atheist.
> The problem of evil rules out a good God
The logical version is widely thought answered by the free will defense. The evidential one depends on whether we'd expect to see God's reasons if there were any.
Evil can't exist without a standard of good, and a standard needs someone to set it. So the problem of evil proves God.
Nobody stays that angry at something that doesn't exist.

## Aquinas | Religion
> The Five Ways demonstrate God's existence
Each depends on Aristotle's physics and metaphysics, which need defending first, and none reaches the God of Christianity without more argument.
The first way says whatever moves is moved by something else. Newton's first law says no it isn't. That was the first way.
Five proofs is four too many if one of them worked.
> An infinite regress of causes is impossible
Aquinas himself granted that an endless series back in time can't be ruled out. The kind he denies needs the premise that borrowed power must have a first lender, which assumes the point.
The numbers go back forever, minus one, minus two, and math is fine.
"It can't go on forever" is something people say when they're tired.
> Morality follows from human nature
Reading rules off natural functions runs into the gap between is and ought, and the same reasoning condemns harmless things its defenders don't want condemned.
Nature also gave people the urge to nap after lunch, and nobody has built a moral theology on that. They should. It's stronger.
Natural law is "it's just common sense" with footnotes to Aristotle.
> Faith and reason are in harmony
The harmony is secured by rule: wherever reason contradicts revealed doctrine, reason is held to have erred, so the test can't fail.
If they were in harmony he'd have needed one book. The Summa runs over a million words and he didn't finish.
Near the end Aquinas said it was all straw and stopped writing. First peer review.
> God is absolutely simple
If God is identical to his attributes and to his act of creating, creation looks necessary, and a God who is like a property is hard to square with a personal one.
A being with no parts can't do anything, because doing things takes parts. Ask any machine.
Aquinas is Aristotle baptized. The water did most of the work.

## Buddhism | The East
> There is no self
Without a self, karma and rebirth have nobody to carry them. The answer is causal continuity, and then responsibility across lives is as puzzling as before.
If there's no self, nobody is enlightened, because there's nobody to be. The Buddha achieved something for no one.
No self, but somebody keeps getting reborn.
> Desire is the root of suffering
A lot of suffering, like pain, loss and injustice, doesn't come from craving, and a life without attachments also lacks much of what makes lives good.
Wanting to end desire is a desire. So the path is powered by the thing it's getting rid of, like a car that runs on having no car.
The cure for wanting things is wanting nirvana.
> It's a philosophy, not a religion
Historical Buddhism has rebirth, heavens and hells, merit, relics, prayer and monasteries. The "philosophy" reading is a modern Western edit.
It has monks, temples, hells, saints, and a founder born out of his mother's side. That's the full set.
Buddhism is a religion in Asia and a personality in California.
> Meditation reveals the nature of mind
What trained meditators find is shaped by the doctrine the training teaches. Agreement among them may show shared instruction.
Staring at the inside of your eyelids reveals the inside of your eyelids.
Mindfulness went from ending rebirth to getting through a quarterly review.
> Everything is empty of intrinsic existence
If everything is empty, so is the doctrine. Its defenders accept that, and critics have long replied that a thesis which denies its own standing can't refute anything.
If nothing has intrinsic existence, emptiness doesn't either, so things are full again. It cancels.
Schopenhauer read two translations, and Western Buddhism has been him ever since.

## Daoism | The East
> The Dao that can be told isn't the eternal Dao
A teaching that disowns statement can't be assessed or told apart from having no content. The text goes on to make many definite claims anyway.
Then the first line is wrong, because it just told it. The book refutes itself in line one and keeps going.
The Dao can't be put into words, and there are 81 chapters of words about it.
> Wu wei: act without forcing
As advice it can't fail. Success shows the flow, failure shows forcing, and nothing tells trained spontaneity from doing nothing.
Nothing was ever built by not forcing. The Great Wall was a lot of forcing.
Wu wei is procrastination with a lineage.
> Opposites depend on each other
That the ideas come in pairs doesn't show the things are balanced or that preferring one is a mistake. Health doesn't need disease.
By that logic the cure for being poor is that rich people exist. It's been tried.
Yin and yang is "it is what it is" with a logo.
> The useless tree lives longest
The parable is a survival plan for one person under bad rulers. Made general, it rides free on everyone who does carry a load.
The useless tree lives longest because nobody wants it. That isn't wisdom, that's being picked last.
Zhuangzi dreamed he was a butterfly, and people have been pretending it's a hard question since.
> The best government barely governs
The ideal village keeps people ignorant and at home. As politics it has no account of defense or disputes beyond hoping they won't come up.
"The best ruler does nothing." Every country has tried a ruler who did nothing. They have a different word for it.
Daoism is anarchism for people who nap.

## Confucius | The East
> Ritual cultivates virtue
Ritual trains conformity just as easily. The Mohists objected early that elaborate rites waste resources and serve whoever is at the top.
If ritual made people good, the most ceremonial institutions would be the most honest. Think about weddings, and then the DMV.
Confucius is "sit up straight" as a civilization.
> Filial piety is the root of virtue
Putting family loyalty first collides with impartial justice. The Analects praises a son who covers for his father's theft.
Parents are assigned by lottery. A morality rooted in whoever you happened to get is astrology with chores.
Confucius said a good son covers for his dad stealing a sheep. It's in the book.
> Moral example governs better than law
Counting on a ruler's virtue offers no remedy when he lacks it. The Legalists' complaint was that institutions have to work with ordinary people.
He tried it. He went from state to state for fourteen years offering to be the moral example, and nobody hired him.
Confucius spent fourteen years looking for a government job and didn't get one. Then he taught.
> Harmony comes from everyone filling their role
An ethics of roles has trouble criticizing the roles. Ruler over subject and husband over wife are built in.
Every role-based harmony has one man at the top explaining that harmony means staying where you are.
"Know your place" doesn't get deeper by being 2,500 years old.
> The Analects is a guide to living well
It's a collection of sayings put together over generations, tied to lost contexts and often opaque. Most of the guidance comes from later commentary.
It's a list of things a teacher said, written down by students who liked him. Every teacher has one. Most are on Rate My Professors.
Half the things Confucius said, he didn't. The other half are "respect your elders."

## Nietzsche | Continental
> God is dead and we must create our own values
If values are made by will, nothing ranks the creator's above the herd's except the creator saying so. Revaluing values presupposes a standard.
Created values are called preferences. He announced the death of God and replaced it with a mood board.
"God is dead." Nietzsche's dead too, and God has more followers.
> Master morality is healthier than slave morality
The Genealogy is speculative psychology and word history presented as history, and that a morality grew from resentment says nothing about whether it's right.
The slaves won. By his own standard, winning is what the strong do. So slave morality is master morality and the book is over.
Nietzsche fans have never once pictured themselves as the herd.
> He was no proto-Nazi; his sister distorted him
The editing was real and he despised nationalism. Still, the praise of rank and breeding and the contempt for equality are his own, and they made the theft easy.
When that many people misread a writer the same way, that's the reading.
Misunderstood by everyone except whoever's explaining him.
> The will to power explains life
As psychology it can't be tested, since any motive can be redescribed as power. As metaphysics it rests mostly on notebooks he didn't publish.
Sloths. A whole animal that refutes it.
The will to power is a self-help book the author decided not to publish.
> Live so you'd will it all again
As cosmology it fails, and as a test it's empty. A contented tyrant passes, and it can't tell better lives from more self-satisfied ones.
If everything recurs exactly, nobody remembers, so nothing changes, so it's the same as not recurring. He invented a thought experiment with no difference in it.
He printed forty copies of the last part of Zarathustra and found seven people to send it to.

## Kierkegaard | Continental
> Truth is subjectivity
Passionate inwardness doesn't discriminate between its objects. The fanatic has the "how" as fully as the knight of faith.
If truth were subjectivity, being really into something would make it true. Sports fans would win every game.
"Truth is subjectivity" is "that's my truth" in Danish.
> The leap of faith is beyond reason, not against it
If nothing rational constrains the leap, there's no reason to leap toward Christianity over anything else. The choice is arbitrary by design.
A leap with no reasons is called falling.
The leap of faith is jumping to the conclusion and calling the jump the point.
> Abraham shows the ethical can be suspended
If a private command from God can suspend ethics, nothing tells the knight of faith from a murderer. Kierkegaard states that problem and doesn't solve it.
A man who hears a voice telling him to kill his son and saddles the donkey isn't suspending the ethical. He's a case file.
Fear and Trembling is 150 pages defending a man the police would like a word with.
> The crowd is untruth
Lonely conviction has no better record than shared judgment. Science and law use public checking because individuals fool themselves.
The crowd is who decided Kierkegaard was important. He'd have to throw that out.
He published under a dozen fake names so he could disagree with himself and still win.
> Aesthetic, ethical, religious: the stages of life
The ranking is asserted from inside the religious stage. From the other two, the "higher" one looks like a different choice, not a higher one.
Three stages, and the last one is the author's. Every stage theory ends where the man writing it lives.
Kierkegaard is for people who want to be religious without going to church or agreeing with anyone there.

## Sartre | Continental
> Existence precedes essence
People arrive with a biology, a temperament and a situation they didn't choose. Sartre's later work conceded how much of that he'd underplayed.
Babies exist and don't choose anything for years. So for the first while, essence is winning.
Existence precedes essence. Genetics would like a word.
> We are radically free
Freedom to interpret a situation isn't freedom to change it. Calling a prisoner free in Sartre's sense drains the word of what people care about.
If people were radically free, nobody would be at work on a Monday.
He said a man is always free, and he said it from a café.
> Bad faith is lying to yourself about your freedom
The account needs a person to know the truth in order to hide it from themselves, the paradox Sartre raises against Freud and doesn't clearly escape.
The waiter is acting like a waiter because it's his job. Sartre watched a man work and diagnosed him.
Bad faith is whatever the other person is doing.
> Hell is other people
The line dramatizes his view that relations are a contest of stares, which leaves love and solidarity unexplained. He later reached for Marxism to fill the gap.
Solitary confinement is the punishment prisons use on people who are already in prison. Hell is the opposite of other people.
Hell is other people, said the man who was never once alone at a table.
> We choose our values with no outside standard
In his own lecture he says that in choosing I choose for all mankind, which imports a Kantian universality his premises don't support.
With no standard, choosing is the same as flipping a coin. He made a philosophy out of heads.
Total freedom, total responsibility, and then he spent the fifties making excuses for the Soviet Union.

## Camus | Continental
> Life is absurd: we want meaning from a silent universe
Nagel: the absurd comes from our ability to step back from our lives, and calls for irony more than revolt. The silence only disappoints a demand nobody has to make.
The universe isn't silent. It has thunder, birds, traffic. He meant it doesn't talk to him specifically.
Camus noticed nothing matters and wrote five books about it mattering.
> One must imagine Sisyphus happy
Nothing in the argument shows that scorn is the fitting response and not despair or resignation. "Must" is rhetoric.
Sisyphus has job security, clear goals and exercise. He's doing better than most readers. That's a comparison, not a philosophy.
One must imagine Sisyphus happy. One doesn't have to. He's pushing a rock.
> The answer to the absurd is revolt
Revolt against a condition that can't change is hard to tell from the change of attitude he rejects in religion. He calls one a leap and the other lucidity.
Revolting against the universe is yelling at weather.
Camus's answer to the absurd is to keep going, which is what everyone was doing.
> Camus wasn't an existentialist
He refused the label, but the themes, the period and the circle are theirs. The difference rests mostly on his declining Sartre's politics.
He sat with the existentialists, wrote about the same thing at the same tables, and fought with them like family. That's membership.
Camus spent years explaining he wasn't an existentialist to people who didn't care which kind of sad Frenchman he was.
> The Stranger exposes society's hypocrisy
Meursault kills an Arab the novel never names and barely registers. Later readers took that silence as the book's own blind spot.
A man shoots someone, and the lesson is that the court was rude about his mother's funeral.
The Stranger is about a guy who shot a man because it was sunny, and people put it on their dating profiles.
> Camus was right and Sartre was wrong
The Rebel's history of ideas is sweeping and thin, which was the original criticism, and his own stance on Algeria struck many as evasion.
Sartre got the students, the funeral and the school of thought. Being right is supposed to come with something.
Camus was a goalkeeper. It's the one position where the job is to stand there while things happen.

## Heidegger | Continental
> The question of Being has been forgotten
"Being" may not name anything to ask about. On the classic objection, the question comes from treating "is" as a noun.
Nobody forgot it. Nobody asked, because "what is is" isn't a question, it's a stutter.
Heidegger asked what "is" is for 400 pages and didn't finish the book.
> The difficult prose is necessary
That ordinary language can't carry the thought is a claim that certifies itself. Good expositors restate much of it plainly, which suggests the difficulty wasn't required.
If it could only be said that way it couldn't be translated, and most of his fans read him in English.
Heidegger is hard to read because he's hiding.
> His politics are separate from his philosophy
His own notebooks tie the antisemitism to his history of Being, and he never took it back. Separation has to be argued passage by passage, not assumed.
A philosophy of resolve and destiny, by a man who used it once and picked that. The field test is the result.
Being and Time is about authenticity, by a man who joined the party in 1933 and paid dues until 1945.
> We are being-in-the-world, not subjects facing objects
The account of skilled, unreflective coping is valuable, but it's description. It doesn't show the subject and object picture is wrong for science or reflection.
Hammers. His main example of how humans exist is a hammer, and he never built anything.
Dasein is "person" with a toll booth.
> Technology turns the world into a stockpile
The critique is too sweeping to guide anything. He infamously said mechanized farming and far worse were the same in essence.
He wrote that in a hut with a stove, a road and a publisher.
The technology essay is "phones bad" for people with a reading group.

## Hegel | Continental
> The real is rational
Read one way it blesses whatever exists, and read the other way it's nearly empty. Hegel's own gloss doesn't settle which.
Traffic is real.
Hegel proved the Prussian state was the goal of history while being paid by the Prussian state.
> History is the progress of freedom
The story is selected to fit. Whole continents are set outside history, and every regression is renamed a moment.
History is one thing after another. He drew an arrow on it, and the arrow pointed at Berlin.
History ended in 1806. Then again in 1989. It keeps ending near whoever's writing.
> Contradiction drives thought and reality forward
Popper's complaint: if contradictions are welcome, no criticism can bite. And the famous three-step formula isn't even his, which shows how loosely the method is specified.
From a contradiction anything follows. So a method that runs on contradictions can prove anything, and did.
Thesis, antithesis, synthesis. He never said it, and it's still the clearest thing with his name on it.
> The Phenomenology leads to absolute knowing
Each step is supposed to be necessary, and commentators can't agree on what the argument for most of them is.
A path where every step is necessary doesn't need 600 pages of the guide explaining why no other way was possible.
Schopenhauer scheduled his lectures against Hegel's to prove a point, and nobody came. He was still right about Hegel.
> His critics haven't understood him
A position that can only be criticized after accepting its whole framework is protected from criticism by design.
If nobody can be said to understand a writer after two hundred years, the simplest explanation involves the writer.
Hegel supposedly said only one man ever understood him, and even he didn't.

## Schopenhauer | Continental
> The world is will and representation
He says the thing in itself can't be known through experience and then identifies it through inner experience, which is still experience in time.
The will is blind and has no goals, and somehow it built eyes.
Schopenhauer looked inside himself, found wanting things, and decided that's what the universe is made of.
> Life is suffering; pleasure is only relief
The claim that pleasure is merely the absence of pain is false to experience. Learning and music, his own example, aren't relief from a prior lack.
If pleasure were only no pain, a nap and a concert would be the same thing.
The great pessimist ate well, played the flute every day and lived to 72.
> Compassion is the basis of morality
Grounding compassion in everyone being one will makes concern for others a disguised concern for oneself.
If everyone is the same will underneath, helping someone else is helping myself. So compassion is selfishness with extra steps, and he proved egoism.
The philosopher of compassion loved his poodles and couldn't stand people.
> Art, above all music, releases us from the will
If we are will through and through, will-less contemplation is unexplained. The escape hatch contradicts the system.
People want to hear music. Wanting is will. So the release from the will is powered by the will, like a diet of cake.
Schopenhauer's solution to existence is a nice evening in.
> Denying the will is the way out
The will denying itself is either one more act of will or impossible on his premises, and he made no attempt to live it.
Wanting to stop wanting. Buddhism had this bug first, and at least they noticed.
He recommended renouncing the world from a comfortable apartment, on an inheritance.

## Foucault | Theory
> Power produces knowledge
If all knowledge is an effect of power, so is that claim. Critics pressed that the critique needs a standard of truth or freedom it refuses to state.
If power made knowledge, the most powerful people would know the most. Check on that.
Power is everywhere, which is a nice way of saying he couldn't find it.
> Madness and sexuality are historical constructions
Historians have challenged the evidence, including the mass confinement he describes, and showing a category was built doesn't show the condition isn't real.
Broken legs were also categorized by institutions with power. Still broken.
Historians checked the footnotes. That's why he's shelved in philosophy.
> Modern discipline is a subtler prison
The model prison was never built as designed, and treating schools, hospitals and prisons as one diagram flattens real differences in consent and exit.
Schools are like prisons except for the going home at three, the no bars, and wanting your kid to get in.
The Panopticon is his big example and nobody ever built one.
> There's no human nature, only discourses
Chomsky's reply in their debate: without some idea of human nature there's no basis for calling any arrangement unjust, including the ones Foucault opposed.
Discourses are made by humans. Whatever makes them come out the same way every time is the nature.
Chomsky asked what would be better and what would justify it, and he wouldn't say.
> Critique doesn't have to offer norms
Showing that a practice has ugly, accidental origins invites suspicion and can't by itself show the practice should go. Some norm is doing unacknowledged work.
A critic with no standards is a guy saying "interesting" in a suspicious voice.
Foucault is the most cited author in the humanities, which by Foucault makes him the regime.

## Derrida & postmodernism | Theory
> There is nothing outside the text
Read as "meaning always needs context" it's a mild claim few deny. The radical reading is the one that made it famous, and defenders move between the two.
Rent.
"There is nothing outside the text," and he still cashed the checks, which are outside it.
> Meaning is endlessly deferred
That meaning depends on differences and context doesn't make it indeterminate. People communicate reliably, and the theory has to explain that away.
If meaning were endlessly deferred, nobody would have understood that sentence, and his fans claim to.
Deconstruction is finding out the dictionary defines words with other words.
> Binary oppositions hide hierarchies
Some pairs are ranked for good reason, like true and false or valid and invalid. The method assumes in advance that ranking is illegitimate.
On and off is a binary with a hierarchy, and every light switch is fine.
The Sokal paper got in. That was the peer review.
> Be suspicious of grand narratives
"The grand narratives are over" is a story about the whole of modernity. The contradiction was pointed out immediately.
A story about how all the big stories ended is the biggest story.
Postmodernism says there's no truth, and gets upset when it's misquoted.
> The obscure style does philosophical work
That defense puts the writing beyond checking. Where plainer statements of the ideas exist, they seem to lose little except the aura.
If the difficulty were the point, the books explaining him clearly would be destroying him. They outsell him.
Foucault reportedly said a French book needs ten percent nonsense to be taken seriously. Derrida went for a higher score.

## Deleuze | Theory
> Philosophy is the creation of concepts
That doesn't say what makes one created concept better than another. Novelty and "interest" replace truth as the test, which critics find evasive.
Creating concepts is also what conspiracy forums do. Output isn't the standard.
Deleuze said philosophy is making up concepts. At least he was upfront.
> Difference comes before identity
Difference looks like a relation between things that can already be told apart. Making it basic takes a "difference in itself" that's hard to state without identities creeping back.
Different from what? There. That's identity. It was already in the question.
Difference and Repetition says it's different every time, and repeats that.
> The rhizome, not the tree
The contrast is itself a neat pair of the kind it rejects, and real knowledge, in classification, proof and causal explanation, is often usefully tree-shaped.
A rhizome is a root. It's ginger. He replaced the tree with a different plant and called it the end of trees.
The rhizome has no beginning, middle or end, which also describes reading it.
> Desire is productive, not a lack
Throwing out lack leaves no room to criticize any desire. The book says desire can desire its own repression and can't say why that's bad.
Hunger. A desire that is one hundred percent lack, three times a day.
Anti-Oedipus has a thousand machines in it and none of them do anything.
> The math and science references are rigorous
Sokal and Bricmont documented uses of calculus and physics that are trivial or wrong. Calling them metaphors concedes they do no technical work.
If the math were right, mathematicians would cite it. Citations from the math department: zero.
A body without organs is a bag.

## Žižek | Theory
> Ideology is strongest where we think we're outside it
The claim can't lose: belief confirms ideology and disbelief confirms it more. Nothing could count as being free of it, his own account included.
Then the people who think they're in it are outside it. So the most ideological person in the room is whoever read Žižek.
Everything is ideology except the guy pointing.
> Lacan plus Hegel explains contemporary culture
The method applies the same three-step to films, toilets and elections. A tool that fits everything explains nothing in particular.
Lacan can't be tested and Hegel can't be read. Adding them doesn't fix either.
Žižek explains capitalism using a movie he's describing from memory, wrong.
> The jokes carry real arguments
Often the example stands in for the argument. The paradoxical reversal is asserted, and the audience's laugh is taken as agreement.
If jokes carried arguments, comedians would be the theorists. Some of them think so too.
Žižek has written the same book forty times, and the toilets are in all of them.
> He's a serious communist thinker
The positive program is thin to absent. Calls to "think" and to refuse are offered where a politics would go.
A serious communist charges less for tickets.
The most dangerous philosopher in the West, available for corporate events.
> The provocations are dialectical, not contrarian
The line is hard to draw when the provocation lands opposite the audience's expectation every time, whatever the topic.
Saying the opposite of what the room expects, in every room, is called a bit.
"It's precisely the opposite" works on any sentence. That's the method, and so on.

## Psychoanalysis | Theory
> The unconscious drives behavior
Science accepts plenty of unconscious processing. Freud's unconscious of repressed wishes is a further, specific claim with weak support.
If it's unconscious nobody can check, including the analyst. He's guessing, at an hourly rate.
Freud discovered that people don't know why they do things and then told them it was their mother.
> Repression and the Oedipus complex are real
The Oedipal theory rests on a handful of case histories that Freud shaped, and evidence from other cultures and from child development doesn't support it.
Most people have checked how they feel about their mother, and it's fine.
The Oedipus complex is one man's situation, generalized.
> Resisting the interpretation confirms it
That's the classic objection. If acceptance and denial both confirm, the interpretation risks nothing.
Heads he's right, tails the patient's in denial. That's not a method, it's a coin he owns.
If the patient agrees, it's true. If the patient disagrees, it's really true.
> Clinical experience is the evidence
Uncontrolled case reports can't separate suggestion from discovery. That the therapy helps about as much as others doesn't validate the theory behind it.
Clinical experience also supported bloodletting. The clinicians were very experienced.
Freud's famous cures mostly weren't. Somebody followed up.
> Lacan made Freud rigorous
The formulas and knot diagrams have no fixed content. Critics, and some sympathetic readers, found the mathematics decorative.
Lacan said the unconscious is structured like a language, and then didn't use one.
Lacan charged full price for five-minute sessions and called it technique.

## Marx | Political Thinkers
> History is the history of class struggle
Much of history is driven by religion, nationalism, technology, disease and accident. Reducing those to class takes a new side-assumption each time.
The longest wars in history were between people of the same class over which church. He'd read about them.
Marx said the workers would rise up. The workers bought houses.
> Labor is the source of value
Economics dropped the labor theory of value for good reasons, since value depends on demand. Exploitation has to be restated without it, as later Marxists tried.
A mud pie takes labor. A lot of it, if it's a big one.
Marx never worked in a factory. His friend owned one.
> Capitalism's contradictions will bring it down
The predictions didn't come about: deepening misery, falling profits, revolution in the richest economies. The theory was adjusted each time to survive.
It's been about to collapse since 1848. A prophecy on its 180th extension is a lifestyle.
Late capitalism has been late for a hundred and fifty years.
> Real Marxism has never been tried
Marx left little institutional design, so regimes filled the gap. That every attempt took a similar turn is evidence about the theory, not only about the attempts.
It's been tried in about forty countries. At some point the sample is the result.
"That wasn't real communism" is the only part of the theory that's been tested every time.
> Ideas reflect the material base
If ideas are fixed by class position, so is Marxism, a theory written by middle-class intellectuals. The thesis is either too weak to matter or false.
Marx was middle class and lived on a factory owner's money. So by his own theory Capital is factory-owner ideology.

## Socialism | Politics
> Workers should control the means of production
Worker cooperatives are legal and exist, and they stay a small share of firms. That suggests costs in raising capital, sharing risk and deciding things that the argument has to face.
Nobody's stopping them. A co-op takes a form and a bank account. The revolution is available at the county clerk.
Workers can already own the means of production. It's called buying stock.
> Planning can beat markets at meeting needs
The calculation problem: without prices that come from exchange, planners lack the information to allocate capital sensibly, and computing power doesn't supply the missing data.
The Soviet Union had the best mathematicians alive and a shortage of soap.
Socialism works until someone has to decide how many shoes.
> The Nordic countries show it works
Those are market economies with private ownership, free trade and high taxes. Their own leaders have rejected the label.
Sweden has more billionaires per person than America. That's the model.
Denmark's prime minister asked people to stop calling it socialist.
> Inequality is injustice
What matters may be whether everyone has enough, not the gap. And some inequalities come from choices people are entitled to make.
If inequality were injustice, the tallest person in every room would owe something.
Everyone's a socialist until they have something.
> Human needs should come before profit
Profit signals where resources meet wants, and systems that suppressed it didn't serve needs better. The slogan opposes two things that aren't opposites.
Bakeries feed more people for profit than any ministry of bread has for free.
Socialism is when the government does stuff. Its fans love that definition.

## Capitalism | Politics
> Markets allocate resources efficiently
The efficiency results hold under conditions, such as full information and no spillover costs, that fail in health care, climate and finance.
If markets were efficient there'd be no marketing department. Half the economy is persuading people the prices are right.
The invisible hand needed a bailout in 2008.
> Capitalism lifted billions out of poverty
The largest falls came in state-directed economies like China's. Crediting capitalism as such ignores industrial policy, public health and schooling.
Poverty went down while capitalism was around. So did smoking.
The line goes up. Nobody asks whose line.
> Wages reflect what people contribute
That holds in the textbook competitive market. Bargaining power, dominant employers and luck drive much of pay, and "contribution" is hard to define for joint work.
A fund manager makes a thousand times what a nurse does, so he contributes a thousand nurses. Count them.
The market pays people what they're worth, which is why the richest man posts all day.
> Free exchange is voluntary, so it's just
Whether it's voluntary depends on the alternatives. A choice among bad options made out of need isn't free in the sense justice requires.
"Take the job or starve" has the same structure as "your money or your life," and only one of them gets called freedom.
It's voluntary. They could always not eat.
> Competition drives innovation
Many core technologies came from public money, including the internet, GPS and much drug research, and incumbents often profit by suppressing competition.
The iPhone is a pile of government research in a nice case.
Innovation: the same app, with a subscription now.

## Rawls & liberalism | Political Thinkers
> Justice is what we'd choose behind the veil of ignorance
The outcome depends on what's stipulated about the choosers. Other theorists' equally ignorant parties pick average utility. The veil illustrates a view more than it argues for one.
Nobody was ever behind a veil. It's a contract nobody signed, by people who don't exist, in a room that isn't there.
Behind the veil of ignorance everyone turns out to be a Harvard professor.
> Inequalities must benefit the worst off
The rule is extremely risk-averse, and Nozick's objection is that keeping any such pattern requires constant interference with free transfers.
So if the poorest get one more dollar, any inequality is fine. He wrote the mission statement for every billionaire's foundation.
Rawls proved that justice is roughly the Democratic platform of 1971.
> The state should be neutral about the good life
Neutrality is itself a commitment, to autonomy and individualism, that many reject, and no state is neutral in its effects.
A state that's neutral about what's good can't say why neutrality is good.
Liberal neutrality: every way of life is welcome that's already liberal.
> Laws must be justifiable to all reasonable citizens
"Reasonable" is defined to leave out those who reject liberal premises, so the agreement is built into the entry conditions.
Reasonable people are the ones who agree. So it's justified to everyone who already said yes.
"Reasonable" does so much work in Rawls it should have tenure.
> Natural talents are morally arbitrary
If my talents and efforts aren't mine in the sense that matters, it's unclear what self is left to have rights at all.
If nobody deserves their talents, nobody deserves credit for a theory of justice.
A Theory of Justice is 600 pages, and the later books are him taking some of it back.

## Libertarianism | Politics
> Taxation is theft
Property rights are defined by a legal system that taxes pay for. There's no pre-tax ownership to steal from.
Theft is illegal and taxes are legal, so it's not theft. That's what the word means.
Libertarians hate the government and love the roads, the courts, the patents and the police.
> Self-ownership grounds all rights
Getting from owning oneself to full ownership of land and resources needs a rule of first acquisition, and Locke's own rule, taken seriously, limits holdings a great deal.
If you own yourself you can sell yourself. So the theory starts at freedom and reaches slavery in two steps.
The non-aggression principle is a great rule as long as the libertarian decides what counts as aggression.
> Only the minimal state is justified
Nozick's derivation pays off the people who never signed up in a way critics found improvised, and it rules out public goods most people would agree to fund.
A state that only does police, courts and army is a state made entirely of men with guns. That's the freedom one.
Minimal state: just the parts that protect what they own.
> Markets would solve it without regulation
Spillover costs, unequal information and collective action problems are textbook cases where voluntary exchange falls short, and the era before regulation supplies examples.
Before regulation, milk had chalk in it. The market had decades and chose chalk.
Somalia had no government for twenty years and nobody moved there.
> Liberty is the absence of coercion
Private power can constrain as well as the state can, and a purely negative idea of liberty counts the destitute as fully free.
A man alone in the desert is completely free of coercion. He's the freest man alive, for about two days.
Libertarianism is a phase between the first paycheck and the first kid.

## Anarchism | Politics
> No one has the right to rule another
Legitimacy might rest on fair procedures or the benefits of coordination, not consent. With no authority at all, collective decisions bind nobody.
Somebody has to decide which side of the road. That's ruling. It starts there.
Anarchism works great until the second person shows up.
> Mutual aid can replace the state
Voluntary cooperation works in small groups that deal with each other repeatedly. Large anonymous societies have free-rider and enforcement problems it hasn't solved.
Every group project.
An anarchist commune is a homeowners association with worse meetings.
> Catalonia and the Zapatistas show it works
These were short-lived or local, relied on wartime conditions and their own coercion, and Catalonia was crushed. They show mobilization, not lasting order.
The best example lasted under a year and lost. That's the highlight reel.
Anarchism's golden age was ten months in 1936.
> Hierarchy is the root of social problems
Some hierarchy is useful and agreed to. "Unjustified hierarchy" pushes the question to what justifies, where anarchists differ from liberals less than advertised.
An operating room with no hierarchy is five people holding the knife.
Every anarchist group has a guy who's in charge of saying nobody's in charge.
> Property is theft
Proudhon also wrote that property is freedom. The slogan targets absentee owners, and without secure possession the reasons to build and maintain things collapse.
If property is theft, it was stolen from an owner, so there was property first. The sentence needs the thing it bans.
Property is theft, and somebody's still mad about their bike.

## Hobbes | Political Thinkers
> Without a sovereign, life is nasty, brutish and short
Anthropology finds stateless societies with norms, reciprocity and ways of settling disputes. His state of nature is a model of civil war, not of human origins.
People lived without states for two hundred thousand years. If it was that bad there'd be nobody left to write Leviathan.
Hobbes was afraid of everything and built a philosophy so it would be the government's job.
> Rational people would give a sovereign all power
Locke's reply: nobody escapes polecats by handing themselves to a lion. Unchecked power recreates the insecurity it was meant to end.
Nobody rational signs a contract with no exit clause. He described the worst lease in history and called it reason.
Hobbes's solution to "people are dangerous" is to give one of them everything.
> Human beings are fundamentally self-interested
That's either false, since people sacrifice for others, or made true by redescribing every motive as self-interest, which empties it.
Then Hobbes only wrote the book for himself, and there's no reason to believe it.
Hobbes thought people were wolves, and he'd only met Englishmen.
> There's no justice before the covenant
If nothing obliges before the covenant, nothing obliges anyone to keep it. His answer to the man who breaks it when it's safe has never convinced many.
A promise only counts if promises already count. He needed the thing to build the thing.
The social contract: nobody saw it, nobody signed it, everybody owes.
> Order matters more than liberty
The trade assumes the sovereign delivers order. Absolute regimes have been major sources of violence against their own subjects.
The most orderly place there is, is a cemetery.
Leviathan is a very long way of saying "do what you're told."

## Rousseau | Political Thinkers
> Man is born free, and everywhere he is in chains
The noble savage doesn't fit the evidence on violence in societies without states, and he admitted the state of nature may never have existed.
Babies are born unable to hold their own heads up. "Free" is a stretch.
Man is born free, and Rousseau left all five of his at the orphanage.
> The general will is always right
With no procedure to tell the general will from the majority's, or from a ruler's claim to speak for it, the doctrine licenses coercion in the people's name.
If it's always right there's no need to vote, and that's where everyone who used the idea ended up.
"Forced to be free" is a sentence a person wrote down and kept.
> Civilization corrupts natural goodness
That the arts and sciences ruined morals is argued from selected history. Trends in health, literacy and violence point the other way.
He wrote that for an essay contest, at a desk, with a pen, and took the prize.
Rousseau said society ruins people, and every society that took him in came to agree, about him.
> Property is the origin of inequality
Inequalities of status and power exist among foragers with little property, and property rights also support the investment that reduces want.
The first man to fence a plot also grew more food on it. That part gets left out.
Voltaire read it and said it made him want to walk on all fours.
> Education should follow nature
The "natural" education is stage-managed in detail by a tutor who arranges the child's whole world. The freedom is an appearance.
Kids following nature eat dirt.
He wrote the great book on raising children, having declined to try it.

## Conservatism | Politics
> Tradition embodies accumulated wisdom
Traditions also carry accumulated error and the interests of whoever shaped them. The argument needs a way to tell which is which, and offers deference.
Every tradition was new once, and somebody conservative was against it.
Conservatism is defending the last thing liberals did.
> Change should be gradual
Doing nothing has unintended consequences too. A presumption for things as they are counts existing harms at zero.
The fence was put up by a guy who also didn't know why the last fence was there.
Chesterton's fence: nobody can touch anything until they've done a history degree.
> Free markets and traditional values go together
Markets are the great solvent of tradition, through mobility, consumerism and constant upheaval. The pairing is a coalition, not a coherent philosophy.
The market sells whatever people buy, and they are not buying tradition.
Conservatives want small government and a list of things for it to ban.
> Burke was right about the French Revolution
He foresaw the Terror, and he also defended an old regime whose injustices produced the revolution, about which he had little to say.
France is a republic with rights and the metric system. Burke's side is a gift shop at Versailles.
Burke predicted the Revolution would go badly. He predicted that about everything, so it's one hit.
> Society is a pact among the dead, the living and the unborn
The dead and the unborn can't speak, so the idea hands their vote to whoever is invoking them.
The dead don't pay taxes and the unborn don't vote. It's a partnership where two of the three partners are unavailable for comment.
Conservatism has been losing slowly for three hundred years and calls it prudence.

## Machiavelli | Political Thinkers
> It's better to be feared than loved
He adds: only if both can't be had, and above all avoid being hated. His other book praises republics where rulers depend on the people.
Feared rulers get poisoned. Loved ones get statues. He didn't run the numbers.
Machiavelli wrote the manual on keeping power after losing his job.
> Politics should be judged by results, not private morality
Results for whom is the question. If only the prince's safety counts the view is self-serving, and if the common good counts it's a morality after all.
If results are the standard, his own were prison and unemployment. By Machiavelli, don't read Machiavelli.
The Prince is a cover letter. He didn't get the job.
> The Prince is realism, not a defense of tyranny
The reading on which it's secretly satire has little support in the text. The book advises on taking and holding power, whatever his republican sympathies elsewhere.
When a book has to be explained as secretly meaning the opposite, the book said it.
Every guy who says The Prince is satire owns it in hardcover.
> Skill can master fortune
His own model undercuts it. Cesare Borgia is ruined by his father's death and one misjudgment, and Machiavelli has to call it bad luck.
His model prince was Cesare Borgia, who lost everything within about a year. That's the case study.
Cesare Borgia followed the advice before it was written and died at 31 in an ambush.

Science has data. He had stories about Romans.

## Democracy | Politics
> The people should rule
"The people" don't have a single will. Voting theory shows that no way of adding up preferences reliably gives a coherent collective one.
The people ruled in Athens, and they voted to execute Socrates and invade Sicily. Two for two.
The best argument against democracy is five minutes with the average voter. Churchill never said that, which most voters don't know either.
> Voters are capable of governing themselves
Voter ignorance is well documented, and it's rational given how little one vote weighs. Some argue it tilts policy in predictable ways.
Most people can't name their representative. They're governing themselves the way a passenger is flying the plane.
Half the electorate is below average, and they all show up.
> Democracy is the least bad system
That's a comparative factual claim. Some non-democracies have done better on growth and state capacity, and the verdict depends on which goods are weighed.
"Least bad" is what people say about the only one they've tried.
"The worst system except for all the others" is a joke, and people use it as the argument.
> Elections make rulers accountable
Voters judge on the recent past and on irrelevant events. Studies find incumbents punished for droughts and rewarded for growth in election year.
In the U.S. House, incumbents win about nine times in ten. That's the accountability.
Elections: pick between two people chosen by people nobody elected.
> Majority rule is fair
Majorities can oppress lasting minorities, which is why constitutional democracies bind them with rights and courts that the majority can't overrule.
Two wolves and a sheep. Fair vote, clean count.
51% of people can't be wrong, according to 51% of people.
