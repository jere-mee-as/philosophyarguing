# Religion, The East, Continental (the original thirteen).

## Arguments for God
> Whatever begins
< The premise is metaphysical: things don't come into being from nothing at all. A cause of space and time would have to be timeless and immaterial, and that's the argument's second stage.
< God didn't begin to exist. The premise says "whatever begins." The universe did, about 13.8 billion years ago, so its application is denied.
< Who made the question? It ends somewhere on every view. Yours ends at "it just is."
> Fine-tuning
< The many-universes reply needs a generator of universes that is itself finely set. And "we lack a distribution" would end a great deal of physics along with this.
< A house is mostly air and plumbing you'd die inside of. Nobody says the house wasn't built for people.
< Someone who survives a firing squad of fifty doesn't say "of course I see them missing, I'm alive."
> A maximally great being
< The modal version doesn't treat existence as a property. It needs only that such a being is possible, and the atheist's reverse premise needs the same kind of defense.
< A greatest possible pizza is like a greatest possible number. There isn't one. Knowledge and power have ceilings and toppings don't.
< Anselm wrote him a reply. The island has no maximum, so it was never the same argument.
> Objective morality
< The standard is God's nature, which is neither above God nor arbitrary. Commands follow from what God is, and that's the third option the dilemma leaves out.
< The claim is about what makes anything wrong, not about who behaves. Atheists can be good. The question is what "good" is doing in that sentence.
< Nobody said they murder. The question is what makes it wrong when nobody's watching anybody.
> Religious experience
< Different witnesses to one event also conflict in the details, and we don't conclude nothing happened. Being able to trigger an experience doesn't show it's of nothing. Vision can be triggered too.
< If millions of sober people across five thousand years reported Elvis, I'd look into Elvis.
< Each one needs you to follow an argument. That's the opposite of faith.

## Atheism
> There's no evidence
< Those arguments are evidence the way a suspect's bad alibi is: something to weigh. Weighed, they point to a first cause at most, and nobody worships that.
< There's no evidence against a teapot orbiting Mars either. It's not fifty-fifty on the teapot.
< I don't have enough faith to believe in yours and the other three thousand.
> The burden
< Fine, whoever asserts carries it. Most atheists assert only that the case hasn't been made, and that's a claim about the arguments they can back up.
< Polytheism was here first. Monotheism turned up second and never did meet its burden with the pagans.
< There are stamp collector conventions too. Not collecting stamps still isn't a hobby.
> Science has made
< Agreed that science doesn't answer why there's anything. Neither does "God did it," which moves the question one step back and calls that an answer.
< Monks also brewed the beer. The inventor's beliefs don't come with the invention.
< Newton also believed in alchemy. We kept the physics.
> Religion does
< Those studies compare the religious with the unreligious inside religious societies. Country by country, the least religious places do at least as well on every measure.
< Those states replaced a church with a party, a prophet and a holy book. They didn't run short of faith.
< And Stalin trained as a priest. Neither fact is an argument.
> The problem of evil
< Free will covers what people do. It doesn't cover the earthquake or the childhood cancer, and "we couldn't see the reason" would excuse any amount of anything.
< I'm using your standard. On your terms, a good God and that hospital ward don't fit. No standard of mine is needed.
< You're not angry at Zeus. You just don't believe in him. Same thing.

## Aquinas
> The Five Ways
< Each needs its metaphysics defended, and it has been, at length. The next hundred questions of the Summa are the further argument to the divine attributes.
< Motion for him means any change, potential becoming actual. Newton's law is about uniform velocity and says nothing against it.
< They're five routes to one conclusion. Nobody says a city is fake because it has five roads in.
> An infinite regress
< A chain where each member only passes on what it has borrowed is the kind that needs a source. A longer line of borrowers doesn't make a lender.
< A hand moves a stick that moves a stone. Add infinitely many sticks and nothing moves without the hand. Numbers don't push anything.
< An infinite train of boxcars doesn't pull itself.
> Morality follows
< The is–ought gap assumes a nature stripped of ends. If a thing has a function, what's good for it follows as a matter of fact, as it does for a heart.
< Rest after a meal is fine by natural law. It's in the tradition under temperance. He's ahead of you again.
< Common sense with footnotes is called a theory.
> Faith and reason
< He holds that truth can't contradict truth, so a conflict means an error in one of the two readings, and that can be the reading of scripture. He says so.
< Each article opens with the strongest objections he could find, stated better than the objectors did. That's the length.
< "Compared to what I've seen." He didn't say it was wrong. He said it wasn't enough.
> God is absolutely simple
< Creation is free because God's one act doesn't depend on its object, so the collapse is denied. And "personal" is said of God by analogy, which the doctrine was built to explain.
< Parts are what need assembling and can fail. Something with no parts can't break and needs nobody to put it together. That's the advantage.
< Baptism is supposed to do most of the work. That's rather the idea.

## Buddhism
> There is no self
< A flame passed from candle to candle is neither the same flame nor a different one. Continuity without an owner is the teaching itself.
< Right. Enlightenment is the end of the idea that somebody needed to achieve something. You've stated the doctrine as an objection.
< A wave keeps arriving too. No particular water travels.
> Desire is the root
< The first arrow is pain and the second is what the mind adds: resistance, grasping, the story about it. The teaching is aimed at the second arrow.
< A raft is for crossing the river. You leave it on the far bank. The desire for the path gets dropped last, and the texts say so.
< And when you get there you put that one down too. It's a ladder.
> It's a philosophy
< Fair, historically. The arguments about self, causation and suffering stand without the cosmology, and the tradition has always graded its own teachings by level.
< Monks, temples and saints is also a description of a university.
< And Christianity is a religion in Rome and a personality in Texas.
> Meditation reveals
< Then compare across traditions with different doctrines. They report the same instabilities of attention and the same passing of sensations, described in rival vocabularies.
< You've looked at them for ten seconds. Try ten days and report back.
< The Buddha taught kings, merchants and soldiers who went back to work. It was always for people with jobs.
> Everything is empty
< He says he holds no thesis, and that emptiness is empty too. It works like medicine that leaves the body along with the illness it cleared.
< Empty means dependent on other things. Emptiness depends on there being things. So it's empty, and nothing fills back up.
< He read two translations and still got the first noble truth right.

## Daoism
> The Dao that can be told
< It says the told Dao isn't the constant one. It doesn't say telling is useless. The words are a finger pointing, and the chapters keep saying so.
< The first line is a warning label. You read the label and then complained there's a product.
< Five thousand characters. It's the shortest scripture there is. He was trying.
> Wu wei
< It's a claim about skill: the cook's knife that never dulls because it goes through the gaps. The difference between forcing and fitting is visible in any craft.
< The Great Wall didn't keep anyone out. Water got around it. That's the example.
< Procrastination is forcing yourself not to. Different thing entirely.
> Opposites depend
< The point isn't that all preferences are mistakes. It's that pushing one side to its limit produces the other, as with strength that becomes brittleness.
< High needs low the way a door needs the empty part. The emptiness is the bit you use.
< It is what it is. You say that like you've found a flaw.
> The useless tree
< Zhuangzi's target is usefulness on other people's terms. The tree gives shade and shelter. It just can't be made into someone's coffin.
< Picked last and still standing. The ones picked first are furniture.
< It is a hard question. You've had 2,300 years.
> The best government
< The ideal is a ruler whose people say "we did it ourselves." As a limit on meddling it's the oldest statement of an idea that later got very popular.
< A ruler who does nothing and has things run well is called a good ruler. The ones you're thinking of did plenty, all of it for themselves.
< Naps are good. That was in the argument.

## Confucius
> Ritual
< He says ritual without feeling is empty, repeatedly. The claim is that shared forms are how feeling gets trained and shown. Even the Mohists kept funerals.
< The DMV has procedure, which is ritual with the meaning taken out. That's what he warned about.
< Sit up straight, look at people when they talk, show up for the dead. It's held up.
> Filial piety
< The same text has him tell sons to remonstrate with a father who does wrong. The sheep passage is about whether a child should be the state's informant.
< Everyone gets a family before they get a theory. Morality has to start where people start. Yours begins with strangers and never arrives home.
< He said the son doesn't testify against his father. Most legal systems now agree. It's called privilege.
> Moral example
< He wanted both. Laws alone make people evasive, and laws plus rulers worth copying give them a sense of shame. The Legalist state lasted fifteen years.
< Nobody hired him, and then his students ran the civil service for two thousand years. Slow start.
< And then he taught. That's where the two thousand years came from.
> Harmony
< Roles bind upward too: a ruler who fails his role stops being a ruler. Mencius drew the conclusion that such a man may be removed.
< He told the men at the top that the name "ruler" has to be earned. That's why none of them hired him.
< Your place includes telling the ruler he's wrong. It's in the job description.
> The Analects
< A book of remembered conversations is the form the teaching took, because it was about situations. The opacity is what any context-bound advice looks like out of context.
< The ones on Rate My Professors didn't get an examination system built on them.
< And you can quote two of them, which is two more than most philosophers get.

## Nietzsche
> God is dead
< He ranks them by a standard he does state: whether a way of valuing strengthens life or sickens it. You can dispute it, and it isn't "because I said so."
< A preference is what you have about soup. A value is what you'd reorganize a life around. He knew the difference, and that was the whole worry.
< He predicted the followers. He said the shadow would be shown in caves for centuries.
> Master morality
< He agrees origins don't settle validity and says so. The history is there to break the assumption that these values have always been obvious.
< Winning by making strength ashamed of itself is the thing he was describing. You've summarized the book and called it a refutation.
< He says the reader is the herd. In the preface. You were warned.
> He was no proto-Nazi
< His aristocratism is of spirit and is aimed at Germans above all. He broke with Wagner over antisemitism and called nationalism a sickness. The theft needed forgery.
< They had to delete and rearrange him to get that reading. A reading that needs scissors is not the reading.
< And the people who "understood" him had to edit the books first.
> The will to power
< In the published books it's mostly a psychological hypothesis, offered against the idea that people chiefly seek pleasure or survival. He put it forward as an experiment.
< A sloth owns its niche so completely that nothing bothers to compete. That's power with no wasted motion.
< The book of that name was assembled by his sister. He'd abandoned the project.
> Live so
< It isn't a test of whether a life is good. It's a test of whether you affirm yours, and a tyrant who passes has shown something about himself.
< If nobody could tell, the only question left is whether you'd say yes. That's the difference. It's in you.
< Seven readers. Now there's a shelf of him in every bookstore, and you're the one counting.

## Kierkegaard
> Truth is subjectivity
< He doesn't say sincerity makes a belief correct. He says that for the questions of how to live, the relation you take is part of what's being asked.
< Fans don't stake their lives on it. Passion in his sense is what's left when being wrong would cost you everything.
< "My truth" asks nothing of you. His asks for all of it.
> The leap
< The leap isn't picking a creed off a shelf. It's what's left when the evidence runs out and living still requires a decision, which is everyone's position.
< Falling is what happens when you didn't choose. He's describing the half-second before, when you could still step back.
< You jumped to that one.
> Abraham
< Right, and that's the book. It was written to make faith terrifying again for readers who thought they had it for the price of a pew.
< That's what his own pseudonym says: either he's a murderer or the ethical isn't the highest. He doesn't let you off with the first.
< The author can't sleep over it either. That's the title.
> The crowd
< The claim is narrower: a crowd can't repent, love or die for you. Where the task is becoming a self, numbers have no vote.
< The crowd also laughed him out of Copenhagen in the papers for a year. He'd tell you which verdict he trusts.
< None of the names agree with each other. That was the method.
> Aesthetic, ethical
< He doesn't argue you up the ladder. Each stage breaks down by its own standards, in boredom or in guilt, and the next is a choice.
< He wrote the first stage so well that people still stop there. Hardly a man rigging it for his own side.
< He spent his last year attacking the church by name. He had the agreeing-with-no-one part covered.

## Sartre
> Existence precedes
< Biology gives a situation. It doesn't give its meaning: whether a limp is a tragedy, a fact or a dare is not in the leg.
< For the first years somebody else is choosing for them. Somebody always is. That's the point.
< Genes gave you a temper. What you do when you're angry is still yours.
> We are radically free
< He means there's no fact about you that makes the choice for you. The prisoner decides what the walls are to him, and that's the freedom nobody can take or give.
< They chose the paycheck over the alternative. That's a choice with a commute.
< He wrote in the café because the apartment had no heat. During the occupation.
> Bad faith
< It works because we are both a set of facts and more than them. One half can be played off against the other, and no second inner person has to be fooled.
< He isn't faulted for working. He's faulted for a little too much flourish, as though being a waiter were all he could ever be.
< Yes. And you can only see it in them because you know the move.
> Hell is other people
< He said later that it means our relations are hell when they're poisoned, since we know ourselves through others' eyes. It was never a recommendation of solitude.
< Solitary is a punishment because the others are still there, deciding it. You've found the play's plot.
< All three people in the play want to leave the room and none of them does. He understood tables.
> We choose our values
< The universality isn't a law from above. It's that choosing shows what you take a human being to be, like it or not.
< A coin doesn't answer for the result. You do. That's the whole difference and it's the theory.
< He got that wrong, and by his own philosophy it's his to answer for. That's the philosophy working.

## Camus
> Life is absurd
< Irony is one response. He's asking whether a person can live with the question open, without pretending it's closed, and irony may be one way of doing that.
< It doesn't answer the question you asked it. That's what silent means.
< Nothing mattering in the end and things mattering now aren't the same claim. The five books are about the second.
> One must imagine
< "Must" means it's the only picture that leaves him anything. Despair hands the rock the win.
< He owns the walk back down. That's the hour Camus cares about, and it's better than most readers get.
< Then he's unhappy and still pushing a rock. You've made it worse and changed nothing.
> The answer
< The difference is what you claim. Religion says the condition is other than it looks. Revolt says it's exactly as it looks and declines to approve.
< You carry an umbrella and you don't say thank you to the rain.
< Doing it knowing is the difference. It's a small one. It's the only one on offer.
> Camus wasn't
< It's more than a label. They hold that we make our essence and he holds there's a human nature worth defending, which is why he wouldn't excuse the camps.
< Sitting at the table isn't signing the manifesto. He's the one who got up.
< He cared. It cost him every friend he had in Paris.
> The Stranger
< The silence is the narrator's, and the book is a portrait of that blankness. An author who grew up poor in Algiers knew what he was showing.
< The court convicts him for not crying at the funeral. That's in the transcript, and that's the point.
< It's a hundred pages. They could finish it before the second date.
> Camus was right
< The history is thin and the judgment was right: no future paradise justifies present murder. On Algeria he refused both sides' terror, and that's a position.
< Sartre got the students. Camus got the verdict of everyone who later counted the dead.
< A goalkeeper is the one who can't blame anyone else.

## Heidegger
> The question of Being
< He begins with that objection. The claim is that we already understand "is" well enough to use it everywhere and can't say what we understand.
< You used it twice in that sentence without trouble and can't define it. That's the question.
< The unfinished half is why it's still being read.
> The difficult prose
< The paraphrases use the tradition's vocabulary, which carries the assumptions he's digging out. Something is lost each time, even where it helps.
< It's been translated twice into English and the translators fought over every term for decades. That's what "can't be said otherwise" looks like.
< He invents a word and defines it on the same page. Hiding is the reverse of that.
> His politics
< Agreed that it must be argued passage by passage. Done that way, the analysis of everyday existence in the 1927 book stands without the politics, and Levinas and Arendt built on it.
< Then judge Frege by his diaries and Aristotle by his slaves. The rule can be applied. Just apply it to everyone.
< It's a 1927 book. His students who had to flee kept teaching it.
> We are being-in-the-world
< It doesn't say the detached view is false. It says that view is derived, a special way of standing back from a world we were already at work in.
< The hammer you don't notice until it breaks. That's the example, and you've never thought about your keyboard either until today.
< "Person" already sneaks in a mind inside a body. The toll is for not doing that.
> Technology
< The remark was grotesque. The analysis is narrower than "technology bad": it's about a way of revealing in which everything shows up as stock to be optimized.
< The stove was for heat. He didn't think of the forest as a wood supply measured in units. That's the distinction.
< "Phones bad" is about the phone. His is about why you check it as if you were inventory.

## Hegel
> The real is rational
< "Real" translates his word for what has worked itself out fully, and he separates it from whatever merely exists. A bad state exists and falls short of that.
< Traffic exists. He said so himself, and it's the distinction: what merely exists against what's actual.
< He'd lost a job under censorship already. The preface is aimed at the people who wanted the state stormed and the people who wanted it worshipped.
> History is the progress
< The geography is indefensible. The thesis survives it: that slavery has gone from unquestioned to unthinkable is hard to describe in any other way.
< The arrow points at the principle that everyone is free. Berlin was only where he was standing.
< He said the principle had been reached, not that events were over. The rest is other people.
> Contradiction drives
< He doesn't assert that P and not-P. A position pressed far enough undermines itself in a specific way, and the next one is what's needed to fix that.
< Explosion is a rule of formal logic. He's describing what happens when a concept is applied, and that follows no such rule.
< He never said it because it's a cartoon of it. That it's clearer tells you about cartoons.
> The Phenomenology
< Each shape of consciousness is tested by its own standard, and fails by it. The reader is supposed to check each step, which is why it can be disputed at all.
< It's 600 pages because he shows each wrong turn failing. A map with only the right road isn't a proof.
< Schopenhauer was right about nobody coming.
> His critics
< Every system asks to be read whole before it's judged. The test is whether, once inside, it explains more than its rivals, and plenty of readers left unconvinced.
< Marx understood him well enough to turn him over. Understanding isn't the problem. Liking it is.
< It's a joke told about every difficult writer. It was told about Kant first.

## Schopenhauer
> The world is will
< He concedes it: inner experience still has the form of time, so the will is the thing in itself under its thinnest veil. He claims that much and no more.
< Eyes are what blind striving looks like after a few hundred million years. He said that forty years before Darwin.
< He looked inside, found wanting, and then found it in the plant, the magnet and you. Check for yourself.
> Life is suffering
< Even granting some positive pleasures, the ledger is his real argument: wants are many and lasting, satisfactions brief, and boredom fills what's left.
< You nap because you were tired and go to the concert because you were bored. Two lacks, two reliefs.
< A man who expected the worst and arranged a tolerable life anyway. That's the philosophy applied.
> Compassion
< Egoism needs a wall between me and you. If the wall is appearance, the self that egoism serves is what has gone, and the concern is left.
< Selfishness with the self removed is called kindness.
< He left his estate to disabled soldiers and their families.
> Art
< He says it's rare and brief, and takes an excess of intellect over what the will needs. An exception at the margin doesn't contradict the rule.
< You want it before it starts. During it, for a few minutes, you don't want anything. That's the release.
< A nice evening in is most of what anyone gets. He just said so.
> Denying the will
< He says he describes sainthood and doesn't practice it, any more than a sculptor of beautiful figures must be beautiful. The possibility is what he argues for.
< It isn't achieved by wanting. It comes from seeing through the thing, and the wanting quiets down. He's explicit that it can't be forced.
< He said a philosopher doesn't have to be a saint. He was right about himself as usual.
