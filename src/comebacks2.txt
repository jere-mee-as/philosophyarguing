## Free will
> I could have done otherwise
< True, the feeling alone proves little. The case rests on deliberation: weighing options presupposes they're open, and nobody can deliberate while believing the outcome is already fixed.
< Every one of those hundred billion times, somebody picked. A hundred billion picks is a hundred billion data points for picking.
< I just didn't. On purpose.
> Without it
< Forward-looking justifications license punishing an innocent person when it deters. Desert is what rules that out, and desert needs an agent who could have done otherwise.
< Courts have an insanity defense because "his brain made him do it" gets a man off. So the law assumes everybody else's brain didn't.
< Aristotle had it 700 years before Augustine, with no God to protect.
> Quantum indeterminacy
< The indeterminism isn't the freedom, it's the room for it. The proposal is that the agent settles what physics leaves open, which is different from a coin settling it.
< A Geiger counter is bigger than a molecule and it clicks for one atom. Amplifying small events is what a brain is built for.
< Determined isn't free either, by your count. So nobody chose to believe you.
> Libet
< Fair demand. One answer is the hard choice where both sets of reasons are the agent's own, so whichever wins is hers and for reasons. It's contested, and it is an account.
< Libet found the conscious veto comes in the last 200 milliseconds. So the brain proposes and I dispose. That's a monarchy, and I'm the king.
< Libet believed in free will. Read the last page.
> I am the source
< Every theory has a primitive. Event causation isn't explained by anything further either, and agents causing things is at least something we seem acquainted with.
< Nobody picks the chessboard or the rules, and the game is still the player's. Starting conditions aren't moves.

## Compatibilism
> Freedom is acting
< The difference is history. A manipulator goes around the agent's own capacity to reflect, and ordinary causes work through it. Versions with a history condition say exactly this.
< A dog can't step back and ask whether it wants to want that. Add that ability and the dog's a person, and yes, then we'd blame the dog.
< The thermostat can't be talked out of it. You can. That's the difference, and it's the only one that matters.
> It's the only free will
< Some revision, yes, as with "solid" after atoms. Tables turned out not to be what people pictured and the word stayed, because what mattered about it survived.
< The other kind is being the uncaused cause of yourself. Nobody wanted that until a philosopher told them they'd lost it.
< Your side's answer, in every book, is a miracle between the ears.
> Determinism doesn't threaten
< The argument's key step is disputed: "not up to us" may not carry over from the past and the laws to what flows through our own deliberation. Being a link in the chain is how anything gets to be up to anyone.
< A domino that can see the others, weigh reasons and stay standing isn't a domino. You've described a person and called it a tile.
< You kept the magic and threw out the person.
> "Could have" means
< Right, the simple conditional fails. Current versions treat ability as a disposition that can be blocked without being lost, the way a glass stays fragile while it's packed in foam.
< Wanting is something I do every day. Wings aren't. One of those ifs is five minutes away.

< I don't blame you. You couldn't have believed otherwise.
> Neuroscience shows

> Accepting it

> Free will is an illusion

## Personal identity
> Psychological continuity
< Fission shows that identity isn't what matters. It doesn't show continuity fails. Both people who walk out have everything worth caring about, and that's the theory's lesson.
< Continuity is a chain. You remember yesterday's you, who remembered the day before. The toddler's in the chain, he's just bad at writing things down.
< You've had those hangovers. You know.
> The teleporter
< The branching case is the odd one. With a single continuer, nothing you care about is missing on arrival, and "death" is being used for an event with no loss in it.
< Banks care about the paper. Nobody cares about your atoms, which is lucky, since you've swapped nearly all of them since your last flight.
< You've loaded a save file. Were you upset?
> Identity isn't
< Parfit grants the feeling and argues it has no object. Once every fact about continuity is known, there's no further fact for the extra concern to be tracking.
< I mind who gets the paycheck because he isn't continuous with me. Give it to my perfect continuer and I'll be thrilled. I'll be him.
< Somebody sufficiently continuous with him signed them.
> You are a human animal
< The animalist says that verdict tracks what we care about, not what we are. Move the cerebrum and an animal gets a new organ with someone's memories in it. Organs transfer. Animals don't.
< A river replaces its water and stays the river. An organism is a process, and the process has one birth certificate.
< The lender agrees with the animalists. So does the bank, the DMV and your mother.
> There's no self
< Causal connection does it: these experiences produce and refer to one another. That's a relation among them, and no extra owner has to be added.
< A whirlpool is water with no string. So is a flame, and a queue. Lots of things hold together by what they're doing.
< And you've discovered nothing, since there's no you. Sorry.

## Modal realism

< They're causally isolated. It's in the definition. A visitor would prove it was the same world all along.
< In some world they didn't. Count yourself lucky to be in this one.
> It's the best analysis

< "Could have won" and "could have been a prime number" both didn't happen. Only one of them is true. Your free analysis can't tell them apart.
< Physics built a whole field for "if." Nobody laughed at that.
> The benefits

< The word is "possible." It's in every sentence of every science.
> "Actual" just means
< Lewis accepts that, and argues our duties were never to all of reality, only to those we can affect. Nothing done here harms anyone elsewhere.
< "Now" used to sound special too. Then physics said it's just where you are in time. "Actual" is next.
< It's a claim about all the worlds, so it's true in all of them or none. You can't shop for it.
> An incredulous stare
< It is some evidence, and he says so. He weighs it against what the theory buys and reports the balance, which is more than a stare does.

## Spinoza
> God and Nature
< The definition was the standard one of his day, and Descartes shared it. He refused the exception Descartes made for created things and followed it through.
< If everything's water, there's still water. "Everything is X" is how you say a lot about X.
< Yes. Pay it with equanimity.
> Everything follows
< He keeps a version of the difference: some things follow from God's nature directly and others through the series of finite causes. Both are necessary, in different ways.
< The arguments were necessary too, and so is whether you're persuaded. Causes work by happening.
< Free of being pushed around by things like this, mostly.
> The geometric method
< There are gaps. The form still earns its keep: every claim lists what it depends on, so a critic can say exactly where to cut. Few philosophers offer that.
< Geometry hasn't needed diagrams since Descartes turned it into algebra. He was up to date.
< You're arguing with it right now. It's holding up.
> Freedom is understanding
< He'd say the ordinary notion was always confused. "Free" meant not being pushed around from outside, and understanding is what turns outside causes into your own reasons.
< A prisoner who understands the lock is called a locksmith.
< Stoicism without the providence. Read to the end.
> Mind and body
< Nothing has to line them up, because there aren't two things. It's one sequence of events described twice. The sameness is the explanation.
< A haircut is a change of mind: now there's the idea of the haircut. Every change in the body has its idea. He wins that example.
< He was expelled, cursed and banned for it. He could have just used a different word.

## Leibniz
> This is the best
< If there were no best, God would have had no reason to create any world, and there is one. And he never claims to show the details, only that it follows from what God is.
< Remove the mosquito and you've removed what eats it, what it pollinates and everything downstream. That's not editing a line, that's writing another world. Show your work.
< Best possible isn't best imaginable. You've imagined worlds. You haven't checked that they're possible.
> Everything has a sufficient reason
< He answers with two kinds of necessity: the reasons for contingent truths incline without compelling, and their analysis never ends. Whether that works is the live question.
< Its reason is that denying it means accepting things for no reason, which is what "unreasonable" means.
< The toddler is right. You just got tired.
> Monads
< It solves a problem everyone had: how separate substances act on each other. He denied that they do and explained the appearance. The rival needed constant miracles, and his needs one.
< Anything with parts is made of something. Follow that down and you reach things without parts or you never stop. He picked the option that stops.
< Your phone stays in sync with a billion others and none of them touch.
> No two things
< If space is real, the spheres differ in where they are. If it isn't, you've described one sphere twice. He'd say you imagined a picture, not a possibility.
< Put them under a microscope. Different scratches. He told you there would be.
< And the courtier didn't find two. Experiment confirmed.
> God permits evil
< He doesn't say each evil buys its own good. The claim is about the whole: this world's laws and its goods come as a package, and a world without the laws loses more.
< The firefighters are in the plan. The best possible world includes people putting fires out.
< Voltaire refuted "everything is nice." Nobody had said that.
> The calculus
< Newton had it and sat on it. The calculus the world learned, with dx, the integral sign and rules a student can apply, is what made it a method.

< Writing it down nicer is what mathematics is.

## Idealism
> To be is to be perceived
< That argument is his weakest. The stronger one: every property we give to matter is known as an appearance, and the supposed thing behind the appearances does no work.
< Neptune was being perceived the whole time, by God. That's the job. It's in the first book.
< And "it exists whether or not anyone's ever aware of it" is what a man says about his novel.
> Matter is
< Matter doesn't explain the order either, it moves it into laws nobody can explain. A mind producing orderly experience is at least a kind of cause we know.
< Dream food doesn't fill you up, and it's nothing but perceptions. Not all perceptions are up to you. That's the line between real and imagined, and he draws it.
< The brick, the head and the pain are all still there. He denied matter, not bricks.
> Consciousness is the one thing
< Right, it takes a second step: everything else is known only as structure, so nothing else we know of could be what the world is in itself.
< The ceiling was an experience of a ceiling. That's what you saw first. You've never seen anything else.
< Solipsism has one mind. This has all of them. That's the opposite.
> We are dissociated
< It is a problem, and an easier one. Minds are known to dissociate, and nobody has ever watched matter turn into experience. One of the two mechanisms exists in nature.
< A brain is what dissociation looks like from outside. You've got the picture and the thing the wrong way round, which is the whole theory.
< The leading rival is that it's all tiny billiard balls that somehow hurt.
> Physics only describes
< It leaves it open, and we have one example of an inner nature, which is our own experience. Extending the only known case is inference, not preference.
< Nobody is acquainted with cosmic soup. We have one sample of what reality is like from the inside, and it isn't soup.
< And materialism comes back every time someone's hungover.

## Physicalism
> Everything is physical
< Take "physical" to mean not fundamentally mental. That isn't tied to today's physics and it isn't empty: it rules out souls and basic experience, which is what the argument is about.
< The periodic table got revised too, and chemistry isn't a subscription to nothing. Updating is what being about the world looks like.
< Whatever physics says, yes. As opposed to whatever you'd like.
> Consciousness is brain activity
< A gap in explanation isn't a gap in nature. "Water" can't be deduced from H2O without knowing how the two concepts connect, and nobody thinks water is something extra.
< Break a radio and the station still plays on every other radio. Break a brain and that person plays nowhere.
< The scan is a picture of it, like a wedding photo. Nobody thinks the photo is the marriage, or that the marriage is a ghost.
> Science will explain
< It's an induction, not a promise: every earlier "science can never explain this" gave way. The burden is on showing this one differs in kind, and the case for that is an intuition.
< Fermat's last theorem took 358 years. It was a theorem the whole time.
< Dualists have been zero years from explaining it since 1641, and still are.
> Mary learns no new fact
< She learns an old fact under a new concept, one that can only be had by having the experience. New way of knowing, same fact.
< Physicists go to concerts for the reason they eat dinner instead of reading the menu. Knowing about isn't having. Nobody said it was.
< And a symphony is air pressure. Okay?
> Causal closure
< It's inductive. Every time a bodily movement has been traced back, the causes were physical. Minds would be the one exception, located in the one place we haven't finished looking.
< The bridge was closed by an engineer's brain state about the math. Find one where the number did it alone.
< It's metaphysics for people who check.

> Physics says

> Micro-experiences
< James's twelve men aren't bonded to each other. The proposals point to specific physical relations, the way mass adds up under some arrangements and not others.
