## Socrates | Ancients
> The unexamined life is not worth living
Plenty of people live decent, meaningful lives without philosophical scrutiny. The claim takes what made his life worthwhile and makes it the test for everyone's.
Examining things is what got him executed, and the people who never examined anything went home to dinner. So the examined life is the one that demonstrably wasn't worth it.
Socrates lost his own trial. If the method worked, he'd have talked his way out.
> He knew that he knew nothing
The modesty is selective. He's confident that virtue is knowledge and that doing wrong is worse than suffering it, which is a lot to know.
If he knew nothing, he didn't know that either, so the statement is false, so he knew something and lied about it. Either way the oracle picked a liar.
"I know that I know nothing," and then he corrected people for fifty years.
> The Socratic method gets at the truth
The early dialogues mostly end in a shrug. The method is good at showing a definition fails and has almost no record of producing one that holds.
A method that worked would finish at some point. Twenty-four centuries later they're still asking what justice is, so the success rate is zero.
The Socratic method is asking "but what is justice?" until the other guy has to go home.
> No one does wrong knowingly
Weakness of will is one of the most familiar things about people. Aristotle thought denying it plainly contradicts what everyone sees.
Everyone who has eaten a second dessert knew. After all, if wrongdoing were only ignorance, a pamphlet would end crime.
"Virtue is knowledge," so nobody ever does wrong on purpose. He'd met Athenians.
> He died for his principles
The sources are admirers writing afterwards, and the Crito's case for obeying the verdict sits badly with the Apology's promise to disobey any ban on philosophy.
He was offered a fine or exile and turned both down, so it wasn't an execution, it was a choice. That's not a martyr, that's a man who wouldn't take the deal.
He picked hemlock over exile to make a point, and the point was Plato's career.

## Plato | Ancients
> The Forms explain what things have in common
The Third Man regress, which Plato raises himself: if a Form explains why large things are alike, something has to explain why they're like the Form.
Things have something in common because people use one word for them. After all, nobody needed a heavenly Chair before the word "chair," and the chairs were fine.
The Forms are just concepts. Plato discovered that words have meanings.
> Philosophers should rule
Even if there are experts on the good, that doesn't settle who checks them. Plato's own late work retreats to the rule of law.
Every profession thinks it should be in charge. Generals propose juntas, priests propose theocracies, and Plato happened to be a philosopher.
Plato wanted philosophers to rule, and he was a philosopher. Nobody designs a utopia where they're the plumber.
> Most people are still in the cave
The allegory assumes a privileged view without any independent way to tell who has left the cave from who has swapped one cave for another.
Everyone who tells the cave story is outside the cave in it. They can't all be outside, so the story shows nobody is.
The cave is a nice story, but The Matrix did it better.
> Learning is recollection
The slave boy in the Meno is steered by leading questions. It shows reasoning can be prompted, which doesn't need a previous life.
If the soul learned everything before birth, it learned it somewhere for the first time. So ordinary learning is possible and the theory isn't needed, by its own account.
Babies have seen the Form of the Good and still can't find their feet.
> Justice is each part doing its own work
That makes justice an inner order, and then a further argument is needed that an ordered soul won't wrong other people. The Republic's bridge between the two is widely thought weak.
A well-run gang has every part doing its job. So by the Republic the Mafia is the most just institution in Italy.
The noble lie: the ideal state runs on propaganda. That's in the plan, not a flaw in it.

## Aristotle | Ancients
> Virtue is the mean between extremes
The doctrine guides nothing unless the right amount is already known, and virtues like justice and honesty don't fit a too-much, too-little scheme.
The middle of two wrong answers is a third wrong answer. Halfway between arson and nothing is still a small fire.
The golden mean says the right answer is always in the middle. Aristotle invented centrism.
> Everything has a telos
Biology now explains apparent purpose by selection, without final causes, and purpose in non-living nature has no explanatory job left.
If everything had a purpose, the appendix would have one. One counterexample is enough, and everyone carries it around.
Teleology is saying the rain falls so the plants can drink.
> Eudaimonia is the human good
The function argument goes from what is distinctive about humans to what is good for them, and being distinctive doesn't obviously carry that weight.
Flourishing took leisure, and leisure took slaves, which he said out loud. So the theory is correct for about four thousand Athenians.
Aristotle said some people are natural slaves, and the virtue ethics crowd just skips that chapter.
> He was the first real empirical scientist
The careful work on marine animals sits beside claims nobody tested for centuries, and his physics resisted exactly the checking he gets praised for.
He looked, and reported that women have fewer teeth. So either looking doesn't work or he didn't look, and both are bad for the father of observation.
Aristotle said heavy things fall faster and nobody checked for 2,000 years. That's the legacy.
> Logic starts with the syllogism
The syllogism covers a small fragment of valid reasoning. It can't handle relations or nested quantifiers, which is why Frege's logic replaced it.
All a syllogism can deliver is what was already in the premises. So two thousand years of logic produced zero new information, by design.
Aristotle is Plato for people who like lists.

## Stoicism | Ancients
> Care only about what's up to you
The line between what is and isn't up to us is blurry. Many things worth caring about are partly in our control, and indifference to them costs something.
Nothing is fully in anyone's control, including their thoughts, which just show up. So by the rule nothing matters, and Stoicism is nihilism with a morning routine.
Marcus Aurelius had the easiest job to be calm in. He owned Rome.
> Virtue is enough for happiness
It follows that the good person is happy on the rack, which Aristotle said nobody would maintain unless they were defending a thesis.
If virtue were enough, virtuous people wouldn't need a philosophy for coping with everything else. The size of the Stoic bookshelf is the refutation.
If nothing external can hurt a Stoic, it's weird how many books they need about it.
> Emotions are judgments, and can be corrected
Feelings often persist against a person's considered judgment, as with phobias, which suggests they aren't simply beliefs.
If emotions were judgments, calm people would be the smartest people. Nobody has ever been talked out of a feeling by hearing it was a logic error.
Stoicism is bottling it up with a Latin name.
> Live according to nature
"Nature" only does the work once it's been read as rational and providential, which is the conclusion the slogan was supposed to support.
Nature gave people anger, fear and grief. So living according to nature means having all three, and the Stoics are the ones going against it.
The Stoics said to live according to nature, and nature is mostly animals screaming.
> Wealth is a preferred indifferent
A class of things that don't matter but are rational to prefer is hard to keep stable. Ancient critics already called it the rival view in different words.
Something preferred is something that matters, because that's what preferring is. So the doctrine is "money matters" with a compliance department.
Seneca wrote about how wealth doesn't matter from inside one of the biggest fortunes in the empire.

## Free will | Metaphysics
> I could have done otherwise
The feeling of open alternatives is just as expected if choice is determined. Introspection doesn't show the absence of causes, only that we can't see them.
Nobody has ever done otherwise. Every person in history did exactly one thing each time, so the evidence is a hundred billion to zero.
"I could have done otherwise." Okay, then do it.
> Without it there's no moral responsibility
That's a reason to want free will, which isn't a reason to think it exists. And holding people responsible can be justified by what it does going forward.
Courts already punish people whose brains made them do it, since that's everyone. So responsibility has worked fine without free will since the first court.
Free will was invented so God wouldn't get blamed for anything. Augustine needed someone to pin it on.
> Quantum indeterminacy leaves room for it
Indeterminism gives randomness, and a random event in a neuron is no more a person's own doing than a determined one.
Quantum effects wash out above the size of a molecule, and a brain is bigger than a molecule. That's the whole physics.
Random isn't free, so quantum mechanics doesn't help, and nothing else is left.
> Libet didn't disprove anything
Granted, the readiness potential is weaker evidence than advertised. Beating one experiment still doesn't supply an account of choice that is neither determined nor random.
The brain started 300 milliseconds before the person knew. Whoever is 300 milliseconds late to their own decision isn't the one making it.
Free will got disproven in the 80s. A guy with an EEG watched the brain decide before the person did.
> I am the source of my own acts
Agent causation posits a self that causes events with nothing about it explaining why then and why that. It names the gap more than it fills it.
Nobody picked their parents, their brain or their first language, and those picked everything else. An uncaused chooser would have to turn up from nowhere at about age four.
Free will is the soul with the serial numbers filed off.

## Compatibilism | Metaphysics
> Freedom is acting on your own desires, unforced
Manipulation cases: if a neuroscientist installs the desires, the agent passes the test and looks unfree, and it's hard to say why ordinary determinism is different.
A dog acts on its own desires, unforced, all day. So either dogs are morally responsible or the definition is missing the entire thing.
A thermostat does what it wants with nothing stopping it. That's compatibilist free will.
> It's the only free will worth wanting
What most people say they want includes being the ultimate source of their choices. Calling the remainder all that was worth wanting is a revision and should be labelled one.
"Worth wanting" is what people say about the thing they got. Nobody describes the prize they were hoping for as "the one worth wanting."
Dennett wrote two books on free will, and the answer both times was "the kind worth wanting," which is the kind that's left.
> Determinism doesn't threaten responsibility
The consequence argument: if our acts follow from the laws and the distant past, and neither is up to us, it's hard to see how the acts are.
If the first domino isn't responsible, the last one isn't either, and it doesn't become responsible by being the one with opinions about it.
Compatibilists keep blame and throw out the only thing that made blame make sense.
> "Could have" means "would have, if I'd wanted to"
The conditional reading fails when the agent couldn't have wanted otherwise. Then the conditional is true and the ability is missing.
And I'd have been a bird if I'd had wings. Any sentence comes out true with an "if" that size.
Compatibilism is redefining "free" until the argument's over.
> Most philosophers are compatibilists
About 59% in the last PhilPapers survey, and survey majorities track training and fashion as well as argument. Headcount doesn't answer the consequence argument.
Most philosophers were dualists once, then idealists, then positivists. The majority view in philosophy has a perfect record of being the last thing everyone believed.
Most philosophers are compatibilists, because most philosophers need both a paycheck and a clear conscience.

## Determinism | Metaphysics
> Every event is fixed by prior causes
Whether the basic laws are deterministic is open. Quantum mechanics is indeterministic on most readings, so the thesis can't just be read off physics.
Physics dropped that a century ago, when it found out particles don't have positions until someone looks. Determinists are defending Newton.
Determinists still look both ways before crossing.
> Nobody deserves blame or praise
Blame, gratitude and resentment are built into relationships in ways that may not be optional. And that desert dies with determinism is itself contested.
Then nobody deserves blame for believing in free will, or for blaming. So the determinist has no complaint about anything anyone does, including punishing people.
Determinists say nobody deserves blame and then get really mad at people who believe in free will.
> Neuroscience shows choices are just brain events
That choices are brain events doesn't show those events aren't choosing. Finding how a capacity works usually isn't finding that it's missing.
Scans show brain events when people do math too. By that logic nobody has ever done math, and the scanners were designed by nobody.
Sapolsky wrote 500 pages on it, so that's done.
> Accepting it makes people more compassionate
The evidence is mixed: some studies found that weakening belief in free will increased cheating, though they replicate poorly. Compassion doesn't follow from a metaphysical thesis either.
If everything's determined, so is who turns out compassionate. So accepting it changes nothing, because the accepting was also on the schedule.
Determinism is fatalism in a lab coat.
> Free will is an illusion we should drop
"Should drop" assumes we can respond to reasons and revise what we think, which is the very capacity compatibilists mean by freedom.
If it's all determined, so is believing in determinism, so there's no reason to think it's true. It's just what the atoms did.
"There's no free will" is always announced by someone who clearly chose to bring it up.

## Personal identity | Metaphysics
> Psychological continuity makes you you
Continuity can branch. In a fission case two people are continuous with one, and identity can't hold between one thing and two.
Nobody remembers being two. So by this theory everyone replaced a toddler and took the body, and that's a lot of unsolved cases.
Psychological continuity means a bad enough hangover is a death.
> The teleporter is just travel
If the original isn't destroyed there are two, and the one who steps out isn't the one who stepped in. That suggests the standard case is a copy plus a death.
A photocopy isn't the original even when it's perfect, which is why banks don't take photocopied money. Same rule for people.
The teleporter obviously kills you. People who say otherwise have never lost a save file.
> Identity isn't what matters
Parfit needs prudential concern to transfer cleanly to a successor. Many find that caring about their own future isn't used up by caring about someone psychologically similar.
If identity didn't matter, people wouldn't mind who collects their paycheck as long as he's similar. Everyone minds.
Parfit said identity doesn't matter and still signed his books.
> You are a human animal
Brain transplant cases cut against it: most people judge that the person goes where the cerebrum goes and the animal stays behind.
The body replaces all its cells every seven years. So if a person is their body, marriages expire, and so do prison sentences.
Every cell gets replaced every seven years, so nobody is the person who took out their student loans.
> There's no self, just a bundle
The bundle needs something that makes these experiences one stream and not scattered ones, and saying what that is tends to bring back what was denied.
A bundle needs something to tie it. Nobody has seen a bundle of sticks with no string, and the string is the self.
Personal identity is philosophers discovering that people change.

## Modal realism | Metaphysics
> Possible worlds are as real as this one
The view buys an analysis of possibility at the price of an ontology most find incredible, and abstract stand-ins for worlds get much of the benefit.
If they were real, somebody from one would have turned up by now. Infinite worlds, infinite people, zero visitors.
David Lewis thought there's a universe with talking donkeys, and Princeton gave him an office.
> It's the best analysis of "could have"
What happens to a counterpart in a sealed-off universe seems irrelevant to what could have happened to me. That's Kripke's Humphrey objection.
"Could have" already means something. It means it didn't. That analysis is free and needs one universe.
"Could have" is a figure of speech. Lewis built a cosmos for it.
> The benefits are worth the ontological cost
Lewis says only kinds of thing count against a theory, not how many. It's unclear why an infinity of concrete universes shouldn't count heavily anyway.
Occam's razor says don't multiply entities, and this multiplies them by infinity. By size of violation it's the most refuted theory there has ever been.
It's the most expensive theory ever proposed. Infinite universes to explain one word.
> "Actual" just means "here"
If "actual" only marks where we are, merely possible suffering is as real as ours, which strains ordinary moral thinking.
"Here" is where I am and "actual" is what exists. One of those is a location. Mixing them up is how you end up paying rent in a counterfactual.
If every possible world exists, there's one where modal realism is false, and it could be this one.
> An incredulous stare isn't an argument
Strong, steady, widely shared disbelief is ordinary evidence in philosophy, the same kind Lewis leans on when he rejects other views for offending common opinion.
If everyone in the room stares at a guy, the stare is data. That's how peer review works.
Lewis said the one objection he couldn't answer was people staring at him. That is an objection.

## Spinoza | Metaphysics
> God and Nature are one substance
The proof runs on definitions of substance and attribute that already rule out more than one. Reject the definition and the monism doesn't follow.
If everything is God then nothing is, the way that if everything's on sale there's no sale. He proved atheism and kept the branding.
Spinoza says everything is God, so my parking ticket is God.
> Everything follows necessarily
If nothing could have been otherwise in any respect, the difference between laws and accidents, which explanation relies on, disappears.
If everything were necessary, so was everyone's opinion of the Ethics, and nobody needed persuading. He wrote arguments anyway.
Spinoza said there's no free will and then wrote a guide to becoming free.
> The geometric method makes it rigorous
The proofs often use premises that aren't among the stated axioms, and the definitions carry the contested content. The form is deductive and the rigor is uneven.
Geometry has diagrams and the Ethics has none. A geometric proof with no shapes is a list with confidence.
The Ethics is laid out like geometry so it looks proven. Nobody argues with a QED.
> Freedom is understanding necessity
Understanding why one is determined is still being determined. Calling that freedom changes the subject from what everyone else means by the word.
A prisoner who understands the lock is still in the cell. Understanding has never opened a door.
Spinoza is Stoicism with axioms.
> Mind and body are one thing, two attributes
With no interaction between the attributes, nothing explains why they line up, and how one substance has really distinct attributes is a standing puzzle.
If mind and body were one thing, a haircut would be a change of mind. Two words, two things. The language settled it.
Spinoza is atheism for people who like the word God.

## Leibniz | Metaphysics
> This is the best of all possible worlds
There may be no best world, as there's no largest number. And that less evil overall was impossible is asserted, not shown.
A world exactly like this one minus one mosquito is possible and better. That took four seconds, and God had eternity.
Leibniz said this is the best possible world. Presumably he hadn't been outside.
> Everything has a sufficient reason
An unrestricted version arguably means nothing is contingent: if every contingent truth has a sufficient reason, so does the whole lot together, and then it couldn't have been otherwise.
Then the principle needs a reason, and that reason needs one. So it's a circle, or a guy saying "because" forever.
The principle of sufficient reason is refusing to accept "it just is." Toddlers have it too.
> Monads are the true substances
Monads that can't interact make causation an appearance and need a pre-established harmony, which explains the coordination by announcing it.
Atoms were found. They have parts and they bump into each other. The Monadology was a prediction and the lab results came in.
Monads have no windows, can't touch each other, and God syncs them like clocks. This was the serious proposal.
> No two things differ only in number
Two exactly alike spheres alone in an empty universe seem possible and seem to be two. Identical particles in quantum physics press the same point.
Two new pennies. Same everything, still two pennies. He could have checked his pocket.
He said no two leaves are alike, and a courtier went into the palace garden to look. That was the experiment.
> God permits evil for a greater good
Greater-good answers struggle with horrors that serve no good in proportion, and with why an all-powerful being needs evil as a tool.
If every evil leads to a greater good, preventing evil makes the world worse. So firefighters are working against God.
Voltaire ended Leibniz with one short novel.
> The calculus we use is his
The notation is his and it won, but Newton got there about a decade earlier. The modern verdict is independent invention, with the rigor supplied by neither.
Newton had it first, and in science first is the only place. Nobody remembers the second man on the moon either.
Newton invented calculus. Leibniz invented writing it down nicer.

## Idealism | Metaphysics
> To be is to be perceived
Berkeley's argument runs together imagining an unseen tree and the tree being imagined. That I have to think of it to picture it doesn't make it depend on a mind.
Things get discovered. If being were being perceived, Neptune began existing in 1846 with a full orbital history already in place.
"To be is to be perceived" is what an influencer believes.
> Matter is an unnecessary posit
Objects that exist apart from minds explain why experience is coherent and shared more simply than a divine mind coordinating everyone does.
Then starving would be a perception problem. People have tried perceiving food.
If everything is mental, a brick to the head is a difference of opinion.
> Consciousness is the one thing we know directly
Granting special access to experience doesn't give that everything is experience. A starting point for knowledge isn't an inventory of what exists.
The first thing anyone sees in the morning is the ceiling. That doesn't make the universe ceiling.
Idealism is solipsism with guests.
> We are dissociated parts of one mind
It inherits a problem as hard as the one it avoids: how one universal subject splits into many private ones is named by analogy and never explained.
Dissociation is something that happens to brains. So the theory needs the universe to have a brain first, which is what it was supposed to explain.
Kastrup says the universe has a dissociative disorder and people are the alters. This is the leading version.
> Physics only describes structure, so the rest is mental
That physics is silent on what things are in themselves leaves the question open. It doesn't favor the mental over the unknown.
Physics also doesn't say the universe isn't soup. A gap in physics is not a vote for the first thing somebody thinks of.
Idealism comes back every time someone does mushrooms.

## Physicalism | Mind
> Everything is physical
Hempel's dilemma: defined by today's physics the thesis is false, and defined by some ideal future physics it's close to empty.
Physics changes every fifty years, so "physical" changes with it. A theory that says everything is whatever the next textbook says is a subscription.
"Physical" just means whatever physics says next year. So physicalism is the view that everything is whatever.
> Consciousness is brain activity
Correlation, and even identity, leave the explanatory gap. Nothing in the neural story shows why this activity comes with experience and not with none.
A radio's music changes when the circuits are damaged, and nobody thinks the band lives in the radio.
A physicalist looks at a brain scan of someone in love and thinks the scan is the love.
> Science will explain it eventually
A promise isn't an argument. Past reductions explained structure and function, and the dispute is whether experience is exhausted by those.
They've had 150 years and every brain ever made to look at. At some point "eventually" is the result.
Physicalists have been twenty years from explaining consciousness since 1950.
> Mary learns no new fact, only an ability
The ability reply seems to leave something out: on seeing red she appears to learn that this is what the others were having, and that's a fact.
If knowing all the physics were knowing everything, physicists wouldn't go to concerts. They'd read the score and stay home.
Physicalism means the experience of red is a voltage. Okay.
> Causal closure rules out the non-physical
Closure is a generalization from cases where consciousness wasn't in question. Using it against non-physical minds assumes the thing at issue.
Numbers aren't physical, and bad math closes bridges all the time. So non-physical things cause plenty.
Physicalism is a metaphysics for people who say they don't do metaphysics.

## Dualism | Mind
> The mind can't be reduced to the brain
A mind that can't be described in brain terms can still be nothing over and above the brain. Plenty of physicalists accept the premise and deny the conclusion.
Weather can't be reduced to one molecule either, and nobody thinks storms are spirits. Well, some people do, and they're also dualists.
Dualism is believing in ghosts, but only one, and it's driving.
> Zombies are conceivable, so physicalism is false
Conceivable may not mean possible. Water without H2O was conceivable too, and the physicalist says the same about zombies.
If zombies were possible, some people would be one, and they'd say exactly what dualists say. So the argument can't rule out its own author.
Zombies are "conceivable." So is a married bachelor if nobody thinks very hard.
> The soul interacts with the body
Princess Elisabeth's question to Descartes still stands: how does something with no size or energy move matter without breaking conservation laws?
Anything that pushes a neuron shows up on an instrument. They've had instruments on neurons for a century and the soul has never tripped one.
Nobody has ever said where the soul gets the energy to push a neuron. Conservation of energy would like a word.
> Mental properties are non-physical properties
If the physical world is causally closed, the extra properties do nothing, including causing anyone to talk about them.
A property is a way of describing a thing. Non-physical properties of a physical thing is like non-wet properties of water.
Property dualism is dualism for people who get embarrassed at parties.
> I can doubt my body but not my mind
The inference fails for ordinary cases: Lois can doubt that Clark flies without doubting that Superman does, and they're still one man.
I can doubt my mind fine. People do it every time they can't find their keys. So mind and body are equally doubtful and the argument's over.
A few beers and the immaterial mind starts slurring.

## Panpsychism | Mind
> Consciousness is fundamental and everywhere
Giving experience to basic physics adds a property with no measurable effect and no agreed way to say which things have how much.
If it were everywhere, anesthesia wouldn't work, because there'd be nowhere to send it.
"Consciousness is fundamental" is giving up in a nice voice.
> It avoids the hard problem
It trades it for the combination problem: how tiny experiences add up to one subject is as unexplained as how neurons produce one.
Saying everything has it isn't explaining it. Ask where money comes from, and "everything's a little bit money" is not economics.
It solves the hard problem by having it everywhere.
> Physics says what matter does, not what it is
The gap doesn't single out experience as the filler. "Proto-experiential" placeholders concede nobody knows what goes there.
What matter does is what it is. A thing that does everything a chair does is a chair. That's all of engineering.
Panpsychism is animism with a grant.
> Micro-experiences combine into minds like ours
Subjects don't seem to add. William James: twelve men each thinking one word don't make a mind thinking the sentence.
A billion grains of sand each being slightly warm don't make a beach with a fever. Adding doesn't work like that.
A billion tiny minds don't make one big mind. A stadium isn't a person.
> Rocks aren't conscious, only their particles
Then the theory owes a rule for which heaps are subjects, and the precise proposals make simple grids of logic gates more conscious than people expect.
If the parts are conscious and the rock isn't, consciousness goes away when more of it is added. No other quantity in physics does that.
Panpsychists say the rock isn't conscious, just its particles. So the rock is a crowd with nobody home, and that's the clarified version.

## The hard problem | Mind
> Explaining function doesn't explain experience
The illusionist reply: what needs explaining is why we judge there's a further ineffable something, and that's a question about function.
Explaining what a heart does explained the heart. There was no second "hard problem of pumping," because nobody insisted on one.
The hard problem is the easy problem plus wonder.
> There's something it's like to be a bat
That we can't imagine being a bat shows a limit on imagination and concepts. It doesn't by itself show a fact beyond the physical ones.
Nobody knows what it's like to be a bat, including the bat, who has never thought about it. A fact nobody has isn't a fact.
"What it's like" isn't a thing. It's a phrase.
> Mary learns something new
She may get a new way of thinking about a fact she already knew, which blocks the step to non-physical facts.
She had every book. If she's surprised, the books were incomplete, and that's a library problem.
Mary learns nothing when she leaves the room. She read the book.
> It's unlike any other scientific problem
People said the same about life. The sense that something is beyond mechanism in principle has a poor track record as evidence.
Every unsolved problem is unlike the solved ones. That's what unsolved means. Then someone solves it and it's chemistry again.
People said life needed a special spark too. Then biology happened and nobody brings it up.
> Qualia are real and irreducible
Dennett's cases suggest people lack steady access to these supposed inner properties. If introspection can't pin them down, they're doubtful as data.
If qualia were ineffable there wouldn't be three thousand papers describing them.
Qualia is a word for feelings so they can be published.

## AI consciousness | Mind
> Current models might be conscious
Their self-reports are learned from human descriptions of experience, so the main evidence is contaminated, and they lack features many theories require.
It predicts the next word. Something that predicts the next word is conscious the way a train schedule is going somewhere.
It's autocomplete. Autocomplete doesn't have a favorite color.
> The substrate doesn't matter, only the computation
That's functionalism, which is the assumption in dispute. Simulating a process isn't producing it, and views tying experience to living bodies are still live.
Computation is something people read into machines. A rock's atoms compute something too under the right description, so either rocks are in or computation is out.
A weather simulation doesn't get anyone wet.
> It passes every test we use on each other
Behavior works as evidence between humans because of shared biology and history. With systems built to produce human-like text, the inference loses that support.
An actor passes every behavioral test for being Hamlet.
A program passed the Turing test in 2014 by pretending to be a 13-year-old. It tests the judges.
> Take model welfare seriously under uncertainty
Precaution needs some estimate of the odds and some idea of what would count as harm. With neither pinned down it can license unlimited concern.
Under uncertainty anything might be conscious, including the spreadsheet with the uncertainty estimates. Nobody is funding spreadsheet welfare.
People thought ELIZA loved them in 1966. Nothing has changed except the budget.
> Brains are just information processing too
That brains process information doesn't show processing is all that matters. The same reasoning would make digestion independent of the stomach.
Brains are also just wet. Nobody thinks a sponge is halfway there.
It's just math. Nobody asks whether long division is lonely.
