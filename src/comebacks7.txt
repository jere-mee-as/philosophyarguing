# Second-wave Metaphysics, Mind, Knowledge, Science, Language & Logic (twenty-eight).

## Eternalism
> Past, present
< The sense of movement is explained by memory: each moment holds traces of earlier ones and none of later ones. Caring more about the future has an evolutionary story too.
< Paris is real and you can't see it from here either. Real doesn't mean reachable from where you're standing.
< "Now" is like "here." The rent is due here too, and nobody thinks other places are unreal.
> Relativity proves
< Not forced, agreed. Every alternative has to add a preferred frame that the physics can't detect, and that's a high price for keeping the present special.
< This is the part of his work he was right about. It's been tested to fourteen decimal places.
< Physics says there's no universal now. The rest is two lines of reasoning.
> The flow of time
< The experience is real and has a place in the block: each moment contains the sense of having come from the last. What's denied is a moving spotlight on top of that.
< A filmstrip contains motion in every frame and the strip isn't going anywhere.
< And they're late at a fixed point in spacetime, forever. It's worse.
> You always exist
< It was never offered as a comfort that outweighs the loss. It's a correction: the dead aren't nothing, they're elsewhere in time, and that's simply true on the view.
< It doesn't help because grief is about now. It's still a fact, and some people do find it steadies them. Einstein wrote it to a widow.
< Last year is a real place. You were there.

## Why there's something
> It's the deepest
< Even if "nothing" isn't a possible state, one can ask why there's this contingent arrangement and not another. The question survives being tidied.
< That explains why anyone notices there's something. Why there is something is still sitting there.
< It produced cosmology. People wanted to know how far back the something goes.
> Physics shows
< Fair, it changes the subject from absolute nothing. It still shows the starting material can be far simpler than anyone guessed, and that narrows the mystery.
< An empty account that fills itself with money for no reason would be worth a book.
< The stuff is as close to nothing as physics allows. Ask for less and you're asking for a void with no properties, which can't be discussed.
> A necessary being
< The universe looks contingent: it began, it changes, it could have had other constants. A necessary foundation would have to be unlike that, which is why it gets a different name.
< The universe started 13.8 billion years ago. Things that have to exist don't have a first day.
< A thing whose nonexistence is impossible. That's a different answer from "a thing."
> It's just a brute fact
< Every explanation ends somewhere. Ending at the totality is at least ending at something we know exists, with nothing extra posited.
< Every explanation ends in "it just is." The only choice is how early you say it.
< From nobody is correct. That's the position.

## Many-worlds
> The wavefunction never
< The branches aren't an added hypothesis. They're what the equation gives if nothing is added, and collapse is the extra, unobserved thing.
< Energy is conserved across the whole wavefunction. Each branch gets its share of the weight. The bill was paid at the start.
< And one who thinks you're funny.
> It's the simplest
< Simplicity is measured in laws. One equation, no exceptions, beats one equation plus a rule for when it switches off that nobody can state.
< Occam counts assumptions. This has one. The stars are numerous too, and nobody shaves those.
< One equation. You can count to one.
> Probability comes
< It's the hard part, and there are two serious derivations of the usual rule. The rivals get their probabilities by putting the rule in by hand.
< Everything happens, in different amounts. A branch with weight 0.9 is nine times the one with 0.1. That's what the number was always measuring.
< In most of them, by weight.
> It just takes
< The others take the formalism and then add a collapse, hidden variables or a rule about observers. This is the reading with nothing added.
< Negative numbers turned out to describe debt, charge and direction. Taking the math seriously has a good record.
< It's the least romantic theory there is. In most branches she said no.

## Mathematical platonism
> Numbers exist
< The access problem assumes knowledge needs a causal link. Logic and morality have no such link either, and we seem to know some of both.
< "Where" is a question about things in space. Ask where the number seven is and you've misunderstood seven.
< Religion says the realm has opinions about you. Numbers don't.
> Mathematics is discovered
< In chess you can change a rule and get another game. Try changing whether there are infinitely many primes.
< Base ten is the spelling. The primes are the same in base two. Nobody invented which numbers are prime.
< The notation is invented. What it's notation for wasn't.
> Science can't do without
< The paraphrase projects have covered a slice of old physics, with heavy machinery. Until one works for quantum theory, the numbers stay on the books.
< The frictionless plane is an idealization you can drop. Try dropping the real numbers and see what's left of the equations.
< The coffee does exist.
> We grasp
< "Intuition" may be the name for what axioms have in common: they force themselves on us. Set theory recovered by finding better axioms, which looks like perception correcting itself.
< That's intuition being corrected by proof, which is the faculty working. Eyes get corrected by measuring, too.
< Every sense works better with training. Ask a radiologist.

> Mathematics is

## Mereological nihilism
> There are no tables

> Ordinary talk
< The story is short: "there's a table" is assertible when things are arranged that way. It's the same move as "the sun rose."
< "The sun rose" is loosely true. You pay for that adverb every morning and don't complain.

> It solves

## Functionalism
> Mental states
< Many functionalists bite it: a system with that organization would have a mind. The intuition against it is the same one that says neurons can't add up to thought.
< If it keeps time, runs for decades and no test can tell, the pawn shop is going by a logo.
< If it walks, talks, plans and grieves like a mind, what's left to ask about?
> The same mind
< Digestion's job is to change chemicals. A mind's job is to process information, and for that kind of job the medium is replaceable. That's the argument.
< Same cake to anyone who eats it blindfolded. Bakers swap ovens all the time.
< A hangover is a hardware fault. Those are in the theory.
> The mind is to
< The analogy is about levels: one process described by what it does and by how it's built. It never needed a literal program file.
< Evolution shipped it, there's no documentation, and the bug reports go to a therapist.
< Each analogy was the best machine of its day, and each was closer than the last.
> Organized the right way
< If two systems are organized identically, any reason to grant experience to one applies to the other. Denying it means experience hangs on something that makes no difference.
< A company isn't organized like a brain. Run it with eighty-six billion staff signaling in milliseconds and we'll talk.
< A billion neurons with ion channels could be in pain. You're fine with that one.

## Illusionism & Dennett
> Consciousness is a kind
< The illusion is a misrepresentation: the brain models its own states as having ineffable inner qualities. A wrong model needs no inner glow to be wrong.
< A camera's autofocus can be fooled and nothing in the camera is having a seeming.
< Explained away is what happened to the life force, and nobody wants it back.
> There's no Cartesian
< Nobody defends a place, and nearly everyone assumes a moment: a line across which content "enters consciousness." That's the theater with the walls removed.
< Then say when a perception becomes conscious, to the millisecond. You'll find you believed in a finish line.
< They say they don't hold it and then ask where it all comes together.
> Qualia don't exist
< What's denied is the theorist's version: private, ineffable, known without error. Pain, taste and color stay, without those four properties.
< Pain hurts, and hurting is a state that makes you stop, wince and remember. I never denied that. I denied a fifth thing.
< Anesthesia stops the processing. That's what they're paid for.
> Explain the reports
< Add the judgments, the discriminations and every disposition, not only the talk. If something remains after all that, say what it does.
< A thermometer has no model of itself as having an inner warmth. You do. Explaining that model is the job.
< The evidence is that people say so. That's all of it, on both sides.

## The Chinese room
> Syntax isn't
< It's a premise, defended by the thought experiment. Anyone who denies it owes an account of how symbol-shuffling comes to be about anything.
< Neurons aren't following rules. They're causing things, like a stomach. The rule-following is our description of them.
< A thought experiment is done by a brain, which is exactly his point about what does the thinking.
> The man in the room
< He answers that: let the man memorize the rules and work in his head. Now he is the whole system and still understands no Chinese.
< He'd learn it by connecting words to the world. From shuffling shapes alone he'd learn the shapes.
< Speed isn't in the argument. Make it instant and nothing changes.
> Simulation isn't
< A simulated calculation counts because we read the symbols as numbers. The meaning is supplied by us, and that's the difference he's pointing at.
< The calculator calculates because we decided what the marks mean. Understanding can't be assigned from outside.
< It's still an argument because you understand it.
> Brains cause
< He never said silicon couldn't. He said running a program is not enough for anything, so whatever does it, does it by its physics.
< Each level has its own causal powers. Chemistry digests and arithmetic doesn't. That isn't a preference.
< Nobody else has said what stuff either. He's the one who admitted it's a question about stuff.

## Extended mind
> Otto's notebook
< Otto doesn't interpret the entry and decide whether to trust it. He reads and goes, as you go on a memory. The role is the same.
< Take away someone's glasses and you've impaired their sight. Nobody finds that paradoxical.

> Cognition doesn't
< The loop is the claim: where the tool is so tightly coupled that taking it out breaks the process, it's part of what's doing the work.
< The fridge isn't digesting. The pencil and paper are doing the long division, since you can't do it without them.
< Yes, and the writing is doing some of the thinking. That's the whole discovery.
> Your phone
< The conditions do the bounding: always available, automatically trusted, easily used. A library you visit twice a year fails them.

> If it'd count

## Behaviorism

< What they think shows up as what they do next. That's the only place anyone has ever found it.
< And both were right.
> Mental talk
< The actor has the outward half. The disposition includes what he'd do if nobody were watching, and there the two differ.

< Secrets are things you'd say under other conditions. That's a disposition.
> Behavior is shaped
< Built-in biases set what can be reinforced. The principle works within them, and every animal trainer and every app uses it.
< They were rewarded for speaking in general, thousands of times. The particular sentence is the generalization.
< Your phone was designed on the pigeon results.
> The inner mind
< Cognitive science kept inner states defined by what they do. That's the behaviorist test, moved inside the skin.
< Your report of the dream this morning is behavior. That's the only access anyone has ever had to it.

## Relativism
> Truth is relative
< A relativist can accept that: the thesis holds in her framework and she recommends the framework. It's uncomfortable and it isn't contradictory.
< True in mine. I'm inviting you in. That's all anyone has ever done.
< That's how it works, yes.
> There's no view
< "Sees more" is judged by some standard, and the standard belongs to a standpoint. That's the point being made again.

> Relativism promotes

## Quine
> There's no line
< That people sort new sentences alike shows a shared habit. It doesn't show the sorting marks a difference in kind, and a habit can be explained by how central a belief is.
< "Momentum is mass times velocity" was a definition until relativity made it false. Which kind of sentence was that?
< Dictionaries get revised whenever the facts change.
> Epistemology should
< The normative part stays as engineering: given the goal of true prediction, which methods work? That's an empirical question.
< A study of which methods of calculating get right answers would tell you how you ought to do it.
< The psychologists who study reasoning and bias took it. It's a field now.
> To be is
< It's a test for what a theory says exists. What does exist is settled by which theory is best, and he never claimed otherwise.
< Things existed before notation. What you're committed to saying exists is in your notation. Different question.
< Sets got in because physics can't be written without them. That's the entrance exam.
> Translation
< Translators converge because they share habits, and the claim is that behavior fixes no more than that. Convergence by habit is what he predicts.
< It means rabbit to a linguist who assumed the native carves the world as he does. That assumption is the finding.
< And every translation you've read was one of several that would have fit.

## Scientific realism
> Our best theories
< The discarded theories were retained in their working parts: Fresnel's equations survived the ether. Realists commit to what does the predicting.
< The earlier ones were approximately right, each more than the last. Newton is still good to eight decimals on a bridge.
< Believed the same core each time, with corrections. That's what approximately true means.
> Otherwise the success
< Selection explains why we have successful theories. It doesn't explain why any theory can predict things nobody had looked for yet.
< Ptolemy fit what had been seen. Nobody pointed a telescope where he said and found a new planet.
< Epicycles never predicted anything new. That's the test, and they fail it.
> Electrons are real
< The picture changed and the handle didn't: the same charge and mass, measured ever more precisely, and you can spray them at a target and things happen.
< A person is a child, then an adult, then old, and stays one person.
< You can't picture a four-dimensional cube either. Unpicturable isn't unreal.
> Science gets closer
< Closeness can be judged from inside: later theories explain why earlier ones worked where they did, and never the reverse.
< Each theory grades the previous one and says where it was off and by how much. That's a key.
< A hiker with a map that keeps getting more of the coastline right.

## Feyerabend
> Anything goes
< Right, it's the conclusion of a reductio. Every rule proposed for science was broken by someone we now call a great scientist, so the rules aren't the essence.
< "Not everything goes" is permitted, as one tradition's house rule. You may keep it at home.
< Two words is the summary of four hundred pages of history. Read the history.
> Galileo won
< The telescope's reliability for the heavens couldn't be established at the time, and the physics that later backed him didn't yet exist. He was right ahead of his evidence.
< They're there now. In 1610 half the people who looked saw nothing or saw double. He won before the instrument did.
< He said the Church had the better argument by the standards of the day. That's a historical claim, and it's hard to refute.
> Science is one
< Smallpox is a real result, and people who want it can choose it. The claim is that citizens should decide which traditions guide their lives.
< They pick it for infections and pick others for dying, raising children and meaning. Free choice is the proposal.
< He saw a traditional healer too, and said it helped. He tried both, which is the position.
> Science should be
< Standards of evidence for a decision are chosen by the people deciding, with experts as advisers. That's already the theory of a jury.
< Juries are laypeople who overrule experts every day. The buildings stand.
< He kept that title because it came from physicists who couldn't answer the history.

## Occam's razor
> The simplest explanation
< There are formal measures, and they explain why the rule works: a model with fewer free knobs fits noise less, so it predicts better.
< "A god did it" explains anything, so it has every knob there is. It only sounds short.
< It usually ends the conversation because there's nothing left to add.
> Don't multiply
< It breaks ties between theories that fit equally well. That's a narrow job, and it does it.
< The elements were needed. "Beyond necessity" is in the rule.
< He wrote four versions. The famous wording is a tidy paraphrase, which is fitting.
> Simplicity is
< A world needn't be simple for the rule to work. With limited data, simpler models make fewer errors on new cases, and that's a theorem.
< Four forces and a handful of particles produce the platypus. The complicated part is downstream.
< Then test it. Simple models win on data they haven't seen.
> It rules out
< It ranks. Where one account needs an added entity and explains nothing more, the entity goes. Whether a case is like that is the argument to have.
< One God with a mind, intentions and unlimited power isn't one simple thing.
< It ends them fine when both people count the same way.

## Scientism
> Science is the only
< Call it a methodological bet backed by a record. Every rival source of knowledge has had centuries to produce a result that holds up.
< It's a generalization from four hundred years of every other method losing. That's evidence.
< It was established in every laboratory, one result at a time.
> Philosophy is dead
< The questions aren't dead. The claim is that the armchair method stopped producing answers once the sciences took its subjects one by one.
< They do it badly and still get further, because they can check.
< And the rest of that book has more to say about time than a century of seminars.
> If it can't be measured
< Those things are tested by use: logic and instruments are kept because the results come out. That's measurement at one remove.
< It's measured by its results. Fields that count things progress and fields that don't go in circles.
< Measuring the wrong thing is bad measurement. The cure is better measurement.
> Ethics can be
< Medicine assumes health is the goal and nobody calls it unscientific. Once you grant that suffering matters, the rest is findings.
< It says which wells kill children. If you need more than that to choose, the problem isn't science.
< Every field assumes its goal. Chemistry assumes you want to know what's in the beaker.

## Frege
> Sense and reference
< Direct reference still has to explain how "Hesperus is Phosphorus" can be news. Whatever does that job is what he called sense.
< The Babylonians didn't know it was Venus and tracked both for centuries. Explaining how that's possible is the theory.
< And that finding it out can take a thousand years. Explain that without him.
> Arithmetic is logic
< The contradiction came from one axiom. Drop it and a single principle about number still yields all of arithmetic, which is most of what he wanted.
< The primes follow from definitions and logic alone, once number is defined. He showed how.
< He answered the letter the same week and printed the problem in his own appendix. That's how it's done.
> Meaning is objective
< The alternative is that the Pythagorean theorem is a different thought in each head. Shared content has to live somewhere other than in individual minds.
< Two toddlers who learn "two" have learned the same thing. Whatever that is, it isn't located in either of them.
< It explains how they can be wrong. Psychology only says what they did.
> He founded
< The ideas are what counted: quantifiers, variables, and a proof as a checkable sequence. Peano and Russell both said where they got them.

> Philosophy should be

## Kripke

< It's what a name is, and for sixty years the standard theory said a name was a disguised description. Someone had to say it.

> Water is necessarily
< He grants that the reference could have been fixed differently. Given that the word picks out this stuff, anything without its makeup isn't it, however it looks.
< Whether water is H2O took a lab. That it couldn't have been anything else, once it is, takes an argument. Two questions.
< "Possible world" is what tells you a fact couldn't have been otherwise. Chemistry doesn't say that part.
> Reference is fixed
< He offered a picture and said it wasn't a theory. The Madagascar case refines it: later intentions to refer can re-anchor a name.

> Nothing fixes

< "I was there" is a fact about feeling certain. The question is what made it plus and not something that agrees on every sum you've done.
< Yes. And nobody has said what makes it adding. That's the result.

## Chomsky
> Language rests
< The list has been pared down to the core: structures built by one recursive operation. A narrower claim is what progress looks like in a science.
< Seven thousand languages and every one has structure-dependent rules and none counts words. That's the universal.
< The data keep agreeing with him. It's awkward for everyone.
> Children hear
< The point isn't quantity. Children never try certain errors that the input doesn't rule out, such as rules based on word order alone.
< Thirty million words and not one saying "the following would be ungrammatical." They know anyway.
< People have counted since. The missing evidence is still missing.
